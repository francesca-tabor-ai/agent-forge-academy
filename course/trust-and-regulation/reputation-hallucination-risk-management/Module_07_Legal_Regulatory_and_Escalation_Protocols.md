## 7.1 When Hallucinations Trigger Legal Exposure

### Legal Risk Categories

**1. False Advertising**

**Triggers:**
- Material misrepresentations about products or services
- False performance claims
- Incorrect pricing or availability
- Misleading comparisons with competitors

**Legal Framework:**
- FTC Act (US) - prohibits deceptive practices
- EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive
- National consumer protection laws
- Industry-specific advertising regulations

**Exposure:**
- Regulatory fines and penalties
- Consumer class action lawsuits
- Competitor lawsuits
- Reputational damage

**2. Product Liability**

**Triggers:**
- Incorrect safety information
- Missing or wrong warnings
- False use-case representations
- Incorrect installation or usage instructions

**Legal Framework:**
- Product liability laws
- Consumer protection regulations
- Industry safety standards
- Tort law (negligence claims)

**Exposure:**
- Personal injury lawsuits
- Product recall requirements
- Regulatory investigations
- Criminal liability (in extreme cases)

**3. Contract Law**

**Triggers:**
- Product descriptions as contractual terms
- Incorrect specifications in contracts
- False availability promises
- Pricing errors in customer communications

**Legal Framework:**
- Contract law principles
- Consumer contract regulations
- E-commerce regulations
- Terms of service enforcement

**Exposure:**
- Breach of contract claims
- Specific performance requirements
- Damages for non-performance
- Contract rescission

**4. Intellectual Property**

**Triggers:**
- Misuse of competitor trademarks
- False endorsement claims
- Incorrect copyright attributions
- Brand misrepresentation

**Legal Framework:**
- Trademark law
- Copyright law
- Right of publicity
- False endorsement laws

**Exposure:**
- Trademark infringement claims
- Copyright violations
- False endorsement lawsuits
- Brand dilution claims

### Materiality Thresholds

**Financial Materiality:**
- Revenue impact threshold (company-specific)
- Customer refund/return costs
- Legal defense costs
- Regulatory fine potential

**Legal Materiality:**
- Violation of specific regulations
- Potential for class action
- Criminal exposure
- Regulatory investigation trigger

**Reputational Materiality:**
- Media attention threshold
- Social media amplification
- Industry impact
- Long-term brand damage

## 7.3 Documentation and Evidence Capture

### Why Documentation Matters

**Legal Protection:**
- Evidence of due diligence
- Defense against negligence claims
- Regulatory compliance proof
- Audit trail for investigations

**Operational Benefits:**
- Learn from incidents
- Improve prevention systems
- Track correction effectiveness
- Demonstrate risk management

### Required Documentation

**1. Incident Documentation**

**Required Information:**
- Date and time of detection
- How error was detected
- Nature of the error
- Severity assessment
- Impact analysis
- Response actions taken
- Correction timeline
- Follow-up actions

**Format:**
- Incident report template
- Timestamped logs
- Screenshots or evidence
- Communication records
- Decision documentation

**2. Decision Documentation**

**Required Information:**
- Who made decisions
- What decisions were made
- Why decisions were made
- When decisions were made
- What alternatives were considered

**Format:**
- Decision logs
- Meeting notes
- Email communications
- Approval workflows
- Escalation documentation

**3. Correction Documentation**

**Required Information:**
- What was corrected
- How correction was made
- When correction occurred
- Who approved correction
- Verification of correction
- Monitoring results

**Format:**
- Correction logs
- Before/after documentation
- Approval records
- Verification evidence
- Monitoring reports

**4. Prevention Documentation**

**Required Information:**
- Prevention measures implemented
- System improvements made
- Process changes
- Training provided
- Monitoring enhancements

**Format:**
- Change logs
- System documentation
- Process documentation
- Training records
- Improvement tracking

### Evidence Capture Best Practices

**1. Timestamp Everything**
- Detection time
- Assessment time
- Decision time
- Correction time
- Verification time

**2. Preserve Original Evidence**
- Screenshots of errors
- Original AI outputs
- Customer communications
- Social media mentions
- Media coverage

**3. Document Chain of Custody**
- Who had access to evidence
- When evidence was accessed
- How evidence was preserved
- Where evidence is stored

**4. Maintain Audit Trails**
- System logs
- Access logs
- Change logs
- Communication logs
- Decision logs

- Criminal exposure
- Complex legal issues
- Regulatory investigation

**Selection Criteria:**
- Industry expertise
- Regulatory experience
- Litigation experience

- Geographic coverage

### Working with Counsel

**1. Early Engagement**

**Benefits:**
- Legal risk assessment

- Regulatory notification guidance
- Documentation requirements
- Privilege protection

**2. Information Sharing**

**What to Share:**
- Incident details
- Documentation
- Internal assessments
- Business context
- Risk tolerance

**What to Protect:**

- Work product privilege
- Confidential business information
- Personal data (privacy)

**3. Response Coordination**

**Roles:**
- Legal: Legal strategy and risk management
- Compliance: Regulatory requirements

**Coordination:**
- Regular updates
- Clear decision authority
- Documented approvals
- Escalation paths

**Triggers:**
- Mandatory reporting requirements
- Material safety issues
- Regulatory violations
- Significant customer impact
- Regulatory inquiry

**2. Notification Process**

**Steps:**
1. Legal and compliance review
2. Determine notification requirements

5. Respond to follow-up inquiries

**3. Regulatory Response**

**Preparation:**

**During Investigation:**

**Resolution:**
- Implement required corrections

- Monitor compliance
- Learn from experience
- Update prevention systems

## 7.5 Escalation Framework

### Escalation Levels

**Level 1: Standard Correction (No Escalation)**

**Criteria:**
- Low severity error
- No legal or regulatory exposure
- Easy correction
- Limited visibility

**Response:**
- Standard correction process
- No legal involvement
- Document incident
- Monitor for patterns

**Level 2: Legal Review (Internal Escalation)**

**Criteria:**
- Medium severity error
- Potential legal exposure
- Regulatory sensitivity
- Customer impact

**Response:**
- Internal legal review
- Risk assessment
- Correction with legal oversight
- Enhanced documentation
- Monitor for escalation

**Level 3: External Counsel (High-Risk Escalation)**

**Criteria:**
- High severity error
- Significant legal exposure
- Regulatory violation risk
- Class action potential

**Response:**
- Engage external counsel
- Comprehensive legal review
- Regulatory notification (if required)
- Crisis management activation
- Full documentation

**Level 4: Regulatory Notification (Critical Escalation)**

**Criteria:**
- Regulatory violation
- Mandatory reporting requirement
- Safety-critical issue
- Significant customer harm

**Response:**
- Immediate legal and compliance review
- Regulatory notification
- Public correction (if required)
- Customer notification
- Full crisis response

### Escalation Decision Tree

```
Error Detected
    │
    ├─ Low Severity?
    │   └─> Level 1: Standard Correction
    │
    ├─ Medium Severity + Legal Risk?
    │   └─> Level 2: Legal Review
    │
    ├─ High Severity + Significant Legal Risk?
    │   └─> Level 3: External Counsel
    │
    └─ Regulatory Violation or Mandatory Reporting?
        └─> Level 4: Regulatory Notification
```
