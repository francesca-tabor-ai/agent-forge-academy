# From Lawyer to Legal Engineer

## Course Overview

**Duration:** 11 weeks  
**Level:** Intermediate to Advanced  
**Time Commitment:** 6-8 hours per week  
**Prerequisites:** Legal background or interest in legal technology; basic understanding of AI/LLMs helpful but not required  
**Access Level:** Professional Access

---

## What You'll Master

By completing this course, you will be able to:

- **Transform Legal**: Transform legal reasoning into system design principles
- **Decompose Legal**: Decompose legal work into machine-executable tasks with proper guardrails
- **how LLMs work and their implications for legal reasoning Understanding**: Understand how LLMs work and their implications for legal reasoning
- **prompts as legal interfaces with proper testing and versioning Development**: Design prompts as legal interfaces with proper testing and versioning
- **retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) Development**: Build retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems for legal knowledge
- **single and multi-agent AI Development**: Create single and multi-agent AI systems for legal use cases
- **Orchestrate Legal**: Orchestrate legal AI systems with proper state management and auditability
- **Evaluate, Test,**: Evaluate, test, and red-team legal AI systems
- **Navigate Ethics,**: Navigate ethics, risk, and professional responsibility in legal AI
- **AI Implementation**: Deploy AI systems in real legal organizations

## Course Modules

### [Module 1: The Legal Engineer Mindset](Module_01_The_Legal_Engineer_Mindset.md)
- From legal reasoning to system design
- What lawyers uniquely contribute to AI systems
- Legal judgment vs. delegated cognition
- Failure modes of naïve legal automation
- **Lab 1:** Analyze a legal workflow and identify automation opportunities

### [Module 2: Decomposing Legal Work into Machine-Executable Tasks](Module_02_Decomposing_Legal_Work_into_Machine_Executable_Tasks.md)
- Translating legal workflows into system components
- Deterministic vs. probabilistic legal tasks
- Task boundaries, escalation points, and human review
- Designing for defensibility, not perfection
- **Lab 2:** Decompose a complex legal task into machine-executable components

### [Module 3: How Large Language Models Actually Work (for Lawyers)](Module_03_How_Large_Language_Models_Actually_Work_for_Lawyers.md)
- What LLMs are and are not
- Tokens, context, embeddings, and temperature
- Hallucination, brittleness, and uncertainty
- Implications for legal reasoning and risk
- **Lab 3:** Experiment with LLM parameters and observe legal reasoning outputs

### [Module 4: Prompting as Legal Interface Design](Module_04_Prompting_as_Legal_Interface_Design.md)
- Prompts as instructions, constraints, and contracts
- Role prompting and structured outputs
- Controlling reasoning and verbosity
- Prompt testing, versioning, and regression
- **Lab 4:** Design and test prompts for a legal use case

### [Module 5: Legal Knowledge Retrieval and Grounding](Module_05_Legal_Knowledge_Retrieval_and_Grounding.md)
- Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
- Working with statutes, cases, contracts, and policies
- Citations, provenance, and traceability
- Avoiding false authority in legal outputs
- **Lab 5:** Build a RAG system for legal document retrieval

### [Module 6: Building Legal AI Agents](Module_06_Building_Legal_AI_Agents.md)
- What makes an agent an agent
- Goals, memory, tools, and planning
- Single-agent legal use cases
- Guardrails for autonomous legal tasks
- **Lab 6:** Build a single-agent legal assistant

### [Module 7: Multi-Agent System Architecture for Law](Module_07_Multi_Agent_System_Architecture_for_Law.md)
- Agent specialization and division of labor
- Researcher, drafter, critic, verifier patterns
- Inter-agent communication and coordination
- Managing disagreement and consensus
- **Lab 7:** Design a multi-agent system for contract review

### [Module 8: Orchestration, State, and Control in Legal Systems](Module_08_Orchestration_State_and_Control_in_Legal_Systems.md)
- Workflow graphs and execution logic
- Deterministic vs. agent-driven routing
- State persistence and auditability
- Designing systems lawyers can explain
- **Lab 8:** Build an orchestrated legal workflow system

### [Module 9: Evaluation, Testing, and Red-Teaming Legal AI](Module_09_Evaluation_Testing_and_Red_Teaming_Legal_AI.md)
- Benchmarks and golden datasets
- Measuring legal accuracy and risk tolerance
- Adversarial testing and edge cases
- Continuous evaluation in production
- **Lab 9:** Create an evaluation framework for legal AI

### [Module 10: Ethics, Risk, and Professional Responsibility](Module_10_Ethics_Risk_and_Professional_Responsibility.md)
- Delegation of legal judgment
- Supervision, accountability, and malpractice risk
- Bias, fairness, and access to justice
- Emerging regulation of legal AI
- **Lab 10:** Conduct a risk assessment for a legal AI system

### [Module 11: Deployment in Legal Organizations](Module_11_Deployment_in_Legal_Organizations.md)
- Integrating AI into real legal workflows
- Buy vs. build decisions
- Change management for lawyers
- Measuring value beyond billable hours
- **Lab 11:** Create a deployment plan for a legal AI system
