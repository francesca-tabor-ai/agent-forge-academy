# Governing the Knowledge Economy: AI Strategy and Implementation

## Course Overview

This course examines the shift from "laissez-faire" governance to a "Knowledge Economy State," exploring how governments must transition from merely coordinating policy to owning and operating strategic AI capabilities. It covers the physical infrastructure of AI (compute and semiconductors), the design of durable institutions (such as DSIT), and the practical implementation of AI in public services.

## Course Description

This course examines the shift from "laissez-faire" governance to a "Knowledge Economy State," exploring how governments must transition from merely coordinating policy to owning and operating strategic AI capabilities. ## Course Structure

### Module 1: The End of Laissez-Faire Governance
**Focus:** Understanding why traditional market-led models are obsolete in the AI era.

**Key Topics:**
- The Shift to General-Purpose State Technology
- The Limits of the Old Model
- The Choice of Governance: Govern capability vs. govern at the margins
- Sovereignty and Dependence

### Module 2: The Physical and Digital Foundations (The Stack)
**Focus:** The material assets required for national AI power.

**Key Topics:**
- Compute as Strategic Infrastructure
- Semiconductors and Supply Chains
- Data Foundations: From open data to strategic assets
- Foundation Models: State-owned or public-interest models

### Module 3: Institutional Architecture and Reform
**Focus:** Designing government structures that endure.

**Key Topics:**
- The Role of DSIT: Operational anchor for Knowledge Economy State
- Combating Institutional Amnesia
- Continuity as Reform
- Moving Beyond "Horizontal Policy"

### Module 4: Economic Alignment and Market Dynamics
**Focus:** Fixing the disconnect between research excellence and national capability.

**Key Topics:**
- The "Great Place to Invent, Poor Place to Scale" Problem
- Venture Capital Limitations
- Hyperscaler Dependency
- Industrial Strategy Implementation

### Module 5: Regulation, Assurance, and Public Trust
**Focus:** Balancing innovation with safety and public confidence.

**Key Topics:**
- The Pro-Innovation Regulatory Approach
- AI Assurance Ecosystems
- Public Attitudes and Trust
- Communicating Value: Human-centric use cases

### Module 6: Operationalizing AI in the Public Sector
**Focus:** Practical application using the government's own tools.

**Key Topics:**
- The AI Playbook
- Procurement as Strategy
- Human-Centric Evaluation
- Upskilling the Civil Service

## Recommended Case Studies

1. **AlphaFold (DeepMind):** A case study in UK-based breakthrough research and its potential for public benefit
2. **NHS AI Lab:** Examining the strategy for AI in health and social care
3. **The Semiconductor Strategy:** Analyzing the UK's £1bn investment vs. the US/EU subsidies
4. **SyRI (The Netherlands):** A cautionary tale of automated decision-making in welfare causing a loss of public trust

## Learning Outcomes

Upon completion of this course, participants will be able to:

1. Understand why traditional market-led governance models fail for AI
2. Recognize the physical and digital foundations required for national AI power
3. Design durable institutional architectures for AI governance
4. Address the disconnect between research excellence and national capability
5. Balance innovation with safety and public trust through regulation and assurance
6. Operationalize AI in the public sector using government tools and frameworks
