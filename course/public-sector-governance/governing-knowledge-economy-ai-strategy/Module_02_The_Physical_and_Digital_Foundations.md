# Module 2: The Physical and Digital Foundations (The Stack)

**Focus:** The material assets required for national AI power.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand compute as strategic infrastructure
- Analyze semiconductor supply chains and UK strategy
- Recognize data as strategic assets, not just open resources
- Evaluate options for foundation models and state ownership

## Core Topics

### Compute as Strategic Infrastructure

**Treating Compute Capacity as a Utility:**

**Not a Commercial Commodity:**
- Compute is strategic infrastructure, not just a service
- Comparable to energy grid or transportation
- Essential for national AI capability
- Requires guaranteed access for public purposes

**Strategic Importance:**
- Large-scale compute needed for AI training
- Limited global supply of high-performance compute
- Concentration in few providers creates risk
- Public sector needs guaranteed access

**Utility Model:**
- Compute as public utility
- Guaranteed access for public purposes
- Strategic reserve capacity
- Public interest prioritization

**UK Approach:**
- National AI Research Resource
- Public compute infrastructure
- Strategic compute capacity
- Guaranteed access for research and public sector

**Challenges:**
- High cost of compute infrastructure
- Rapid technological change
- Balancing public and private needs
- International competition

### Semiconductors and Supply Chains

**Examining the UK's Semiconductor Strategy:**

**UK's Position:**
- Strong in chip design (ARM, etc.)
- Weak in manufacturing
- Dependence on foreign manufacturing
- Strategic vulnerability

**Risks of Reliance on Taiwan (TSMC):**
- Geopolitical risks
- Supply chain concentration
- Potential disruption
- Strategic dependence

**Chip Design vs. Manufacturing:**
- **Design:** UK has strengths (ARM architecture)
- **Manufacturing:** UK lacks capability
- **Gap:** Design without manufacturing creates dependence
- **Strategy:** Need to address manufacturing gap

**UK Semiconductor Strategy:**
- £1bn investment announced
- Focus on specific strengths
- International partnerships
- Comparison to US/EU subsidies (much smaller)

**Challenges:**
- Scale of investment needed
- International competition
- Long-term commitment required
- Balancing design and manufacturing

### Data Foundations

**The Shift from "Open Data" to Strategic Data Assets:**

**From Open Data to Strategic Assets:**
- Data is strategic resource, not just public good
- Public sector data has strategic value
- Must be leveraged for public benefit
- Cannot be ceded to private actors

**Strategic Data Assets:**
- Health data (NHS)
- Transport data
- Education data
- Government operations data

**Public Leverage:**
- Data under public control
- Used for public benefit
- Strategic advantage
- Not ceded to private actors

**Integration Requirements:**
- Data integration across departments
- Strategic data management
- Public data platforms
- Controlled access and use

**Challenges:**
- Data silos and fragmentation
- Privacy and security concerns
- Technical integration
- Governance and control

### Foundation Models

**The Risks of Total Reliance on Foreign Models:**

**Current Dependence:**
- Reliance on US foundation models (GPT, Claude, etc.)
- Foreign control of critical AI infrastructure
- Strategic vulnerability
- Policy autonomy constraints

**Risks:**
- Supply chain disruption
- Strategic leverage
- Policy constraints
- National security concerns

**Options for State-Owned or Public-Interest Foundation Models:**

**Option 1: State-Owned Models**
- Government develops and owns models
- Full control and autonomy
- High cost and complexity
- Requires significant capability

**Option 2: Public-Interest Models**
- Public-private partnerships
- Public interest governance
- Shared investment
- Balanced control

**Option 3: Open Source Models**
- Community-developed models
- Reduced dependence
- Lower cost
- Less control

**UK Approach:**
- Exploring public-interest models
- Partnerships with industry
- Strategic investments
- Balance between autonomy and efficiency

## The Stack Concept

**Understanding the Full Stack:**
- **Compute:** Infrastructure layer
- **Semiconductors:** Hardware layer
- **Data:** Input layer
- **Models:** Intelligence layer
- **Applications:** Use layer

**Strategic Control:**
- Need control at multiple layers
- Cannot rely on single layer
- Strategic depth required
- Diversification important

## Key Takeaways

1. **Compute is strategic:** Must be treated as infrastructure, not commodity
2. **Semiconductors matter:** Manufacturing gap creates strategic vulnerability
3. **Data is strategic:** Must be leveraged for public benefit, not ceded
4. **Foundation models are critical:** Cannot rely entirely on foreign models

## Reflection Questions

1. How should compute be treated as strategic infrastructure, and what does this mean in practice?
2. What are the risks of the UK's semiconductor strategy, and how can they be mitigated?
3. How does the shift from "open data" to "strategic data assets" change data governance?
4. What are the options for foundation models, and which approach should the UK take?
5. How do the different layers of "the stack" work together, and where should the state focus?
