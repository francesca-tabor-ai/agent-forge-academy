# Module 6: Ethics, Safety, and Accountability

**Objective:** To establish governance structures that ensure public trust and safety.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the role of the AI Safety Institute
- Comply with Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard (ATRS)
- Manage bias and discrimination in AI systems
- Defend against security risks including data poisoning and prompt injection

## Core Topics

### 6.1 The AI Safety Institute

**Role and Purpose:**
- State-led testing of frontier AI models
- Independent evaluation of AI safety
- Research and development of safety standards
- International collaboration and coordination

**Key Activities:**
- **Model Testing:** Testing frontier models for safety and risks
- **Risk Assessment:** Assessing capabilities and risks
- **Research:** Developing safety techniques and standards
- **International Engagement:** Collaborating with international partners

**Bletchley Declaration:**
- International agreement on AI safety
- Commitment to safe AI development
- Collaboration on AI governance
- UK leadership role

**Testing Frontier Models:**
- Evaluating capabilities and risks
- Identifying potential harms
- Assessing safety measures
- Providing independent assessment

**International Collaboration:**
- Sharing research and findings
- Coordinating safety standards
- Joint testing initiatives
- Best practice exchange

**Governance Implications:**
- Independent safety assessment
- Evidence-based policy
- International coordination
- Public trust and confidence

### 6.2 Algorithmic Transparency

**Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard (ATRS):**
- Standard for disclosing AI use in decision-making
- Required for public sector AI systems
- Promotes transparency and accountability
- Builds public trust

**Key Requirements:**
- **System Description:** What the system does
- **Data:** What data is used
- **Logic:** How decisions are made
- **Outcomes:** What the system affects
- **Governance:** Who is responsible

**Compliance:**
- Mandatory for public sector AI
- Public disclosure of AI use
- Regular updates required
- Accessible format

**Benefits:**
- Increased transparency
- Public trust
- Accountability
- Scrutiny and oversight

**Challenges:**
- Balancing transparency with security
- Technical complexity
- Resource requirements
- Keeping records up to date

**Implementation:**
- Create ATRS records
- Publish publicly
- Regular updates
- Training and support

### 6.3 Managing Bias and Discrimination

**Types of Bias:**

**Representational Bias:**
- Training data underrepresents certain groups
- Historical discrimination encoded in data
- Lack of diversity in datasets
- Geographic or demographic gaps

**Algorithmic Bias:**
- Models learn and amplify biases
- Discriminatory patterns in outputs
- Unfair treatment of protected groups
- Perpetuation of inequalities

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- **Data Quality:** Ensure representative datasets
- **Bias Testing:** Test for discriminatory outcomes
- **Fairness Metrics:** Measure and monitor fairness
- **Diverse Teams:** Include diverse perspectives
- **Regular Review:** Ongoing bias assessment

**Equality Act 2010 Compliance:**
- Prohibits discrimination on protected characteristics
- Applies to AI systems
- Requires fair treatment
- Allows positive action where appropriate

**Protected Characteristics:**
- Age
- Disability
- Gender reassignment
- Marriage and civil partnership
- Pregnancy and maternity
- Race
- Religion or belief
- Sex
- Sexual orientation

**Compliance Requirements:**
- Non-discriminatory outcomes
- Fair treatment
- Reasonable adjustments
- Monitoring and review

**Governance:**
- Bias assessment in development
- Ongoing monitoring
- Regular review
- Remediation when needed

### 6.4 Security Risks

**Data Poisoning:**
- Malicious manipulation of training data
- Corrupting model behavior
- Introducing backdoors or vulnerabilities
- Difficult to detect

**Prevention:**
- Data validation and verification
- Secure data sources
- Monitoring for anomalies
- Regular model retraining

**Prompt Injection:**
- Manipulating AI through malicious inputs
- Bypassing safety measures
- Extracting sensitive information
- Causing harmful outputs

**Prevention:**
- Input validation
- Prompt sanitization
- Output filtering
- Security testing

**Model Inversion:**
- Reconstructing training data from model outputs
- Privacy breaches
- Data extraction
- Confidentiality risks

**Prevention:**
- Differential privacy
- Data minimization
- Access controls
- Output restrictions

**Other Security Risks:**
- **Adversarial Attacks:** Manipulating inputs to cause errors
- **Model Theft:** Stealing proprietary models
- **System Compromise:** Breaching AI systems
- **Supply Chain Attacks:** Compromising AI supply chain

**Security Best Practices:**
- Secure development lifecycle
- Regular security testing
- Access controls and authentication
- Monitoring and incident response
- Supply chain security

## Key Takeaways

1. **Safety is paramount:** AI Safety Institute provides independent assessment
2. **Transparency builds trust:** ATRS promotes accountability and public confidence
3. **Bias must be actively managed:** Requires ongoing assessment and mitigation
4. **Security is critical:** Multiple attack vectors require comprehensive defense
