# Module 4: Evidence, Ethics, and the Digital Divide

**This module covers the immediate risks of AI in the courtroom and the broader societal implications.**

## Learning Objectives

- Understand deepfakes and AI-generated evidence challenges
- Analyze admissibility of AI-generated materials under evidence rules
- Recognize access to justice opportunities and digital divide risks
- Evaluate algorithmic bias and fairness in legal AI tools

## Core Topics

### 4.1 AI in the Courtroom – Deepfakes and Evidence

**The Challenge of AI-Generated Evidence:**

**Deepfakes:**
- AI-generated images, video, audio
- Highly realistic
- Difficult to detect
- Increasingly sophisticated

**Detection Arms Race:**
- Generation improves
- Detection tries to keep up
- Constant evolution
- No perfect solution

**The Challenge:**
- Difficulty detecting AI-generated evidence
- Realistic appearance
- Authentication problems
- Trust issues

**Admissibility Under Federal Rules of Evidence:**

**Relevance:**
- Must be relevant to case
- Probative value
- Material to issues
- Standard applies

**Authentication:**
- Must prove authenticity
- Source verification
- Chain of custody
- Technical challenges

**Prejudice vs. Probative Value:**
- Balance risks
- Unfair prejudice
- Misleading potential
- Judicial discretion

**Case Studies:**
- **Unacknowledged AI Evidence:** Passed off as real
- **AI-Generated Witness Testimony:** Fabricated statements
- **AI-Generated Voicemails:** Fake audio evidence
- **Deepfake Video:** Fabricated visual evidence

**Judicial Response:**
- Apply existing rules
- Require authentication
- Scrutinize carefully
- Protect integrity

### 4.2 Access to Justice vs. The Digital Divide

**The Opportunity: AI Chatbots for Unrepresented Litigants:**

**Access Tools:**
- **NyayGuru:** Legal assistance chatbot
- **Roxanne AI:** Housing and repair help
- **Legal aid chatbots:** Navigate systems
- **Self-service tools:** Empower users

**Benefits:**
- Help unrepresented litigants
- Navigate complex systems
- Access to information
- Reduced barriers

**The Risk: The Digital Divide:**

**Big Law Advantage:**
- Expensive, verified tools
- High-quality AI systems
- Professional support
- Competitive advantage

**Legal Aid Services:**
- Generic, risky models
- Limited resources
- Basic tools
- Disadvantaged position

**The Divide:**
- **Haves:** Advanced AI tools
- **Have-nots:** Basic or no tools
- **Inequality:** Widening gap
- **Justice:** Unequal access

**Mitigation:**
- Public investment
- Quality tools for all
- Training and support
- Equal access

### 4.3 Algorithmic Bias and Fairness

**Recidivism Tools: COMPAS Critique:**

**COMPAS Tool:**
- Predicts recidivism risk
- Used in sentencing
- Algorithmic assessment
- Controversial results

**The Critique:**
- Entrenches historical biases
- Based on arrest data
- Reflects discrimination
- Unfair outcomes

**Bias Sources:**
- Historical arrest patterns
- Discriminatory practices
- Skewed training data
- Reinforced inequalities

**Impact:**
- Unfair risk scores
- Discriminatory outcomes
- Justice compromised
- Trust eroded

**Facial Recognition: Misidentification Risks:**

**The Problem:**
- Misidentification in underrepresented populations
- Training data gaps
- Accuracy varies by group
- False positives

**The "Invisible Mask" Example:**
- Certain populations less recognized
- Lower accuracy rates
- Higher false positive risk
- Discriminatory impact

**Why This Matters:**
- Wrongful identification
- False arrests
- Injustice
- Trust issues

**Mitigation:**
- Diverse training data
- Regular auditing
- Transparency
- Human oversight

## Key Takeaways

1. **Deepfakes challenge:** AI-generated evidence is difficult to detect, requiring careful authentication under evidence rules
2. **Access opportunity:** AI chatbots (NyayGuru, Roxanne AI) help unrepresented litigants but risk widening digital divide
3. **Digital divide:** Big Law with expensive tools vs. legal aid with generic models creates inequality
4. **Algorithmic bias:** Tools like COMPAS and facial recognition may entrench historical biases and misidentify underrepresented populations

## Reflection Questions

1. How do judges apply Federal Rules of Evidence (relevance, authentication, prejudice) to AI-generated materials?
2. How do AI chatbots improve access to justice while potentially widening the digital divide?
3. Why do recidivism tools like COMPAS risk entrenching historical biases found in arrest data?
4. How does facial recognition misidentification affect underrepresented populations, and how do you mitigate this?
5. How do you balance access to justice opportunities with digital divide risks?
