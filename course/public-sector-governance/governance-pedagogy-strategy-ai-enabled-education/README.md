# Governance, Pedagogy, and Strategy in the AI-Enabled Education State

## Course Overview

This course examines the transition from standardized schooling models to AI-enabled adaptive learning systems. It covers practical applications for reducing teacher workload, the ethical management of data and privacy, and the strategic rebuilding of state capacity to govern artificial intelligence in education.

## Course Description

This course explores how AI is transforming education from standardized industrial models to personalized adaptive learning. It covers the historical context, practical applications, assessment challenges, risk governance, and strategic implementation for school leaders and policymakers.

## Course Structure

### Module 1: The Historical Context and the "AI Reckoning"
**Focus:** How AI destabilizes the traditional standardized model of education.

**Key Topics:**
- The Human Capital State: Evolution from social good to economic instrument
- The Limits of Standardisation: Age-based cohorts, uniform curricula, high-stakes testing
- The "AI Reckoning": Shift from delivery systems to stewardship and public learning infrastructure
- Global Context: South Korea's AI textbooks, China's adaptive tutoring centers

### Module 2: Practical Applications and Pedagogy
**Focus:** Current use cases in UK schools, from administrative automation to personalized learning.

**Key Topics:**
- Teacher Workload Reduction: Lesson planning, resource creation, administrative automation (Oak National Academy's Aila)
- Adaptive Tutoring and Personalisation: Intelligent Tutoring Systems (Third Space Learning's Skye)
- SEND and Accessibility: Text-to-speech, translation, scaffolding for neurodiverse learners
- The "Human in the Loop": Ensuring AI enhances teacher-student relationships

### Module 3: Assessment, Integrity, and Curriculum
**Focus:** Addressing disruption of traditional testing and evolution of curriculum design.

**Key Topics:**
- The End of Standardised Testing?: Shift to continuous AI-driven assessment
- Malpractice and Academic Integrity: Limitations of AI text detectors, AI-resilient methods (oral vivas, in-person verification)
- Curriculum Adaptation: Integrating AI literacy, prompt engineering, critical evaluation
- Intellectual Property: Copyright laws, AI-generated content, student data in training

### Module 4: Risk, Governance, and Data Ethics
**Focus:** Legal and ethical frameworks required to deploy AI safely.

**Key Topics:**
- Data Privacy and GDPR: Data extraction risks, corporate capture, GDPR-compliant whitelists
- The "Pre-Mortem" Strategy: Risk assessment imagining project failure
- Bias and Accuracy: Hallucinations, Westernised biases, historical biases
- Age Appropriateness: Age restrictions (18+, 13+), homework policies

### Module 5: Strategic Implementation for Leaders
**Focus:** Managing the change process for school leaders and policymakers.

**Key Topics:**
- The "AI Champion" Model: Identifying and empowering staff to drive adoption
- Bridging the Digital Divide: Addressing inequity of access to paid AI tools
- Procurement and Infrastructure: Public infrastructure, interoperability, preventing vendor lock-in
- Rebuilding State Capacity: Productive State model, DBT and DfE coordination

## Learning Outcomes

Upon completion of this course, participants will be able to:

1. Understand the historical evolution of education and how AI challenges standardized models
2. Apply practical AI tools for teacher workload reduction and personalized learning
3. Design AI-resilient assessments and integrate AI literacy into curriculum
4. Implement risk governance and data ethics frameworks for safe AI deployment
5. Lead strategic implementation of AI in education with equity and state capacity focus
