# Module 4: Risk, Governance, and Data Ethics

**Focus:** The legal and ethical frameworks required to deploy AI safely.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand data privacy and GDPR requirements
- Implement pre-mortem risk assessment strategies
- Address bias and accuracy concerns
- Manage age appropriateness and homework policies

## Core Topics

### Unit 4.1: Data Privacy and GDPR

**Understanding Risks and Establishing Compliance:**

**Data Extraction Risks:**
- AI tools may extract student data
- Privacy concerns
- Data security risks
- Unauthorized access

**Corporate Capture:**
- Student learning data captured by companies
- Commercial use of data
- Loss of control
- Privacy violations

**GDPR Compliance:**
- Legal requirements
- Data protection principles
- Consent requirements
- Rights of data subjects

**Establishing Whitelists:**
- GDPR-compliant tools only
- Approved tool lists
- Regular review
- Compliance verification

**Key Requirements:**
- **Lawful Basis:** Legal basis for processing
- **Consent:** Appropriate consent
- **Purpose Limitation:** Specific purposes only
- **Data Minimization:** Minimum necessary data
- **Security:** Appropriate security measures

### Unit 4.2: The "Pre-Mortem" Strategy

**Implementing Risk Assessment:**

**Pre-Mortem Process:**
- Imagine project failed
- Work backward to identify causes
- Proactive risk identification
- Preventive action

**How It Works:**
1. Assume project failed
2. Identify failure causes
3. Assess likelihood and impact
4. Develop mitigation strategies
5. Implement preventive measures

**Risk Categories:**
- **Bias:** Algorithmic bias risks
- **Safety:** Student safety concerns
- **Privacy:** Data protection risks
- **Accuracy:** Hallucination and errors

**Application:**
- Before AI deployment
- Regular risk reviews
- Team-based process
- Documented assessment

**Benefits:**
- Proactive risk management
- Early problem identification
- Better decision-making
- Reduced harm

### Unit 4.3: Bias and Accuracy

**Addressing AI Limitations:**

**Hallucinations:**
- AI generates false information
- High confidence in errors
- Particularly risky in education
- Must be identified and corrected

**Westernised Biases:**
- Training data reflects Western perspectives
- May exclude other cultures
- Reinforces dominant narratives
- Limits diverse perspectives

**Historical Biases:**
- Training data reflects historical bias
- May perpetuate discrimination
- Reinforces inequalities
- Must be actively addressed

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- Diverse training data
- Bias testing and monitoring
- Regular review
- Human oversight

**Accuracy Concerns:**
- Verify AI outputs
- Human review required
- Critical evaluation
- Error correction

### Unit 4.4: Age Appropriateness

**Managing Age Restrictions and Homework Policies:**

**Age Restrictions:**
- Many AI tools are 18+ or 13+ with permission
- Age verification required
- Parental consent needed
- Compliance essential

**Common Restrictions:**
- **18+:** Many AI tools require adult users
- **13+ with Permission:** Parental consent required
- **School Use:** May have different rules
- **Compliance:** Must follow terms of service

**Homework Policies:**
- Unsupervised AI use at home
- Difficult to control
- Policy needed
- Guidance required

**Policy Considerations:**
- What AI use is allowed?
- What requires supervision?
- How to verify work?
- How to assess learning?

**Implementation:**
- Clear policies
- Student and parent communication
- Training and guidance
- Monitoring and enforcement

## Risk and Governance Framework

**Key Elements:**
- **Data Protection:** GDPR compliance and privacy
- **Risk Assessment:** Pre-mortem strategies
- **Bias Mitigation:** Active identification and correction
- **Age Compliance:** Appropriate restrictions and policies

## Key Takeaways

1. **GDPR essential:** Data privacy and protection must be prioritized
2. **Pre-mortem helps:** Proactive risk identification prevents problems
3. **Bias must be managed:** Hallucinations and cultural bias require active mitigation
4. **Age matters:** Age restrictions and homework policies must be addressed

1. What are the data privacy risks of AI in education, and how can GDPR compliance be ensured?
2. How does the pre-mortem strategy help identify and prevent AI risks?
3. How can AI hallucinations and biases be identified and mitigated in educational contexts?
4. How do you manage age restrictions and homework policies for AI use?
5. What governance structures are needed to ensure safe and ethical AI deployment in education?
