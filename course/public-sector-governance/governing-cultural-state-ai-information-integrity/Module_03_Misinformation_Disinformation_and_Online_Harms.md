# Module 3: Misinformation, Disinformation, and Online Harms

**This module focuses on the specific harms exacerbated by AI and social media business models, distinguishing between illegal content and "legal but harmful" material.**

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the business model of the web and its incentives
- Analyze generative AI risks in creating misinformation
- Evaluate case studies of election interference and political influence operations
- Recognize challenges in regulating health misinformation

## Core Topics

### The Business Model of the Web

**Analysis of Advertising-Driven Models:**

**Attention Economics:**
- Maximize engagement
- Keep users scrolling
- Increase time on platform
- Optimize for clicks

**Advertising-Driven:**
- Revenue from ads
- More engagement = more revenue
- Incentivize viral content
- Divisions drive engagement

**Incentives:**
- Spread misinformation
- Promote divisive content
- Amplify extremes
- Maximize engagement

**The Problem:**
- Misaligned incentives
- Harmful content profitable
- Truth not rewarded
- Engagement over accuracy

**Impact:**
- Misinformation spreads
- Social division increases
- Trust erodes
- Harm amplified

### Generative AI Risks

**The Role of AI in Creating Harmful Content:**

**Hallucinations:**
- AI generates false information
- Appears plausible
- Difficult to detect
- Spreads rapidly

**False Citations:**
- Fabricated sources
- Fake references
- Misleading authority
- Credibility undermined

**Hyper-Realistic Synthetic Media:**
- Deepfakes
- Realistic fake videos
- Audio manipulation
- Visual deception

**Political Influence:**
- Misinformation campaigns
- Election interference
- Manipulation
- Democratic risks

**Why This Matters:**
- Scale of generation
- Speed of spread
- Realistic appearance
- Hard to detect

### Case Study: Election Interference

**Reviewing AI Tools for Political Influence:**

**2025 Anti-Labour Videos:**
- AI-generated content
- False stories
- Political figures targeted
- YouTube platform

**Political Influence Operations:**
- Bot networks
- Coordinated campaigns
- False narratives
- Manipulation

**Mechanisms:**
- AI-generated content
- Automated distribution
- Coordinated timing
- Targeted audiences

**Impact:**
- Electoral interference
- Democratic legitimacy
- Public trust
- Political outcomes

**Lessons:**
- AI amplifies risks
- Scale increases threat
- Detection difficult
- Governance needed

### Health Misinformation

**Challenges of Regulating Harmful Health Content:**

**The Problem:**
- Harmful health claims
- Medical misinformation
- Public health risks
- Real-world harm

**Regulatory Challenges:**
- Distinguish misinformation from satire
- Separate harmful from educational
- Context matters
- Nuance required

**Limitations of Automated Systems:**
- Cannot understand context
- Miss nuance
- Over-block legitimate content
- Under-block harmful content

**Examples:**
- Vaccine misinformation
- Medical scams
- Health conspiracies
- Treatment falsehoods

**The Balance:**
- Protect public health
- Preserve free expression
- Allow education
- Prevent harm

## Key Takeaways

1. **Business model:** Advertising-driven "attention economics" incentivizes spread of misinformation and divisive content
2. **Generative AI risks:** AI creates hallucinations, false citations, and hyper-realistic synthetic media for political influence
3. **Election interference:** Case study of 2025 anti-Labour videos and political influence operations via bot networks
4. **Health misinformation:** Challenges distinguishing harmful health content from satire/education, limitations of automated systems

## Reflection Questions

1. How does the advertising-driven business model of the web incentivize the spread of misinformation?
2. What are the specific risks of generative AI in creating hallucinations, false citations, and deepfakes?
3. How do AI tools enable election interference, and what are the mechanisms of political influence operations?
4. What are the challenges in regulating health misinformation, and why do automated systems struggle?
5. How do you distinguish between illegal content and "legal but harmful" material, and what are the implications?
