# Governing the Cultural State: AI, Information Integrity, and Digital Regulation

## Course Overview

This course examines the transformation of state governance in the age of artificial intelligence, with a specific focus on the United Kingdom's Department for Culture, Media and Sport (DCMS) and its evolution into a "Cultural State". Students will explore how AI challenges traditional regulatory models by collapsing the distinctions between content creation and distribution, necessitating a shift from content oversight to "information integrity". The curriculum covers the rise of platform power, the challenges of misinformation and copyright, and the legislative frameworks emerging to manage systemic risks.

## Course Description

This course examines the transformation of state governance in the age of artificial intelligence, with a specific focus on the United Kingdom's Department for Culture, Media and Sport (DCMS) and its evolution into a "Cultural State".

Students will explore how AI challenges traditional regulatory models by collapsing the distinctions between content creation and distribution, necessitating a shift from content oversight to "information integrity". The curriculum covers the rise of platform power, the challenges of misinformation and copyright, and the legislative frameworks emerging to manage systemic risks.

## Course Structure

### Module 1: The Evolution of the Cultural State
**This module traces the historical trajectory of the DCMS from a ministry of heritage to a central node of digital governance.**

**Key Topics:**
- From Heritage to Code: Expansion from preservation and arts to overseeing global platforms and digital infrastructure
- The Digital Awakening: Initial view of digital as economic "plumbing" led to regulatory lag
- Industrial Alignment: "Creative industries" narrative framed tech firms as enablers, obscuring governance challenges
- The Accidental Digital Department: DCMS became de facto steward of information environment, managing culture as infrastructural power

### Module 2: The AI Inflection Point
**This module analyzes how artificial intelligence fundamentally alters the nature of digital governance problems, rendering traditional tools obsolete.**

**Key Topics:**
- Machine-Generated Reality: AI autonomously produces text, images, video, collapsing creation/distribution boundary
- Scale, Speed, and Opacity: Traditional moderation (takedowns, harm lists) fails with real-time, personalized, opaque AI content
- The "Liar's Dividend": Prevalence of AI/deepfakes allows real documentation to be dismissed as fake, eroding trust
- Algorithmic Radicalization: Recommendation algorithms direct users toward increasingly extreme or ideologically biased content

### Module 3: Misinformation, Disinformation, and Online Harms
**This module focuses on the specific harms exacerbated by AI and social media business models, distinguishing between illegal content and "legal but harmful" material.**

**Key Topics:**
- The Business Model of the Web: Advertising-driven "attention economics" incentivizes spread of misinformation and divisive content
- Generative AI Risks: AI creates hallucinations, false citations, and hyper-realistic synthetic media for political influence
- Case Study: Election Interference: 2025 anti-Labour videos and political influence operations via bot networks
- Health Misinformation: Challenges distinguishing harmful health content from satire/education, limitations of automated systems

### Module 4: The UK Regulatory Framework (The Online Safety Act)
**This module provides a detailed critique of the UK's legislative response to digital harms, focusing on the Online Safety Act 2023.**

**Key Topics:**
- Duties of Care: Act focuses on systems and processes rather than individual content pieces
- Scope and Categorization: Distinction between illegal content, content harmful to children, and Category 1 (high reach/risk) services
- Enforcement Powers: Ofcom can issue fines (up to £18m or 10% revenue) and business disruption measures
- Criticism and Limitations: Threats to encryption, privacy, freedom of expression, and potential failure to address cross-platform disinformation

### Module 5: Copyright, Creative Industries, and AI Training
**This module explores the economic and legal tensions between AI developers and the creative sectors regarding the use of data for model training.**

**Key Topics:**
- The Remuneration Debate: AI companies argue for broad text/data mining exceptions, creatives demand payment and revenue sharing
- Transparency of Inputs: Granular transparency of training data needed to assess risks (CSAM) and copyright infringement
- Opt-Out Mechanisms: Current tools like robots.txt have technical and practical limitations, not universally effective
- Sovereign AI and Open Source: Debate over UK building public service models vs. relying on US proprietary systems, open source security implications

### Module 6: The Future of Information Governance
**This module outlines the necessary institutional reforms for the "Cultural State" to effectively govern in the 21st century.**

**Key Topics:**
- From Content Oversight to Information Integrity: Shift to upstream focus on incentives, architectures, and integrity-by-design
- Institutional Reform: DCMS as "systems steward" coordinating across Ofcom, CMA, ICO
- Technical Solutions and Standards: Watermarking, metadata (C2PA), and provenance standards establish trust online
- Media Literacy as Defence: State-funded programs build public resilience against AI-generated misinformation

- **Duration:** ~8-10 weeks

## Learning Outcomes

Upon completion of this course, participants will be able to:

1. Understand how DCMS evolved from heritage ministry to digital governance steward
2. Analyze how AI fundamentally alters digital governance problems and renders traditional tools obsolete
3. Evaluate specific harms of misinformation, disinformation, and online harms exacerbated by AI
4. Critique the UK's Online Safety Act and its approach to digital regulation
5. Navigate tensions between AI developers and creative industries regarding copyright and training data
6. Design institutional reforms for effective information governance in the 21st century

## Course Philosophy

This course emphasizes:
- **Institutional Evolution:** Understanding how DCMS became accidental digital department
- **AI Transformation:** Recognizing how AI fundamentally changes governance challenges
- **Systems Thinking:** Moving from content oversight to information integrity and systems stewardship
- **Democratic Values:** Balancing safety, privacy, freedom of expression, and democratic legitimacy
