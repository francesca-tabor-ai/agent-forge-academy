# Module 5: Building and Buying AI Solutions

**Objective:** Practical guidance for project managers, commercial leads, and technical teams.

## Learning Objectives

- Identify high-value AI use cases
- Make informed "build vs. buy" decisions
- Navigate routes to market including CCS frameworks
- Assemble effective multidisciplinary teams

## Core Topics

### 5.1 Identifying Use Cases

**High-Value Opportunities:**

**Speeding Up Service Delivery:**
- Automating routine tasks
- Reducing processing times
- Improving efficiency
- Better user experience

**Examples:**
- Automated form processing
- Document classification
- Customer service chatbots
- Appointment scheduling

**Predictive Analytics:**
- Forecasting demand
- Risk prediction
- Resource optimization
- Early intervention

**Examples:**
- Demand forecasting for services
- Fraud detection
- Risk assessment
- Predictive maintenance

**Use Cases to Avoid:**

**Fully Automated High-Risk Decisions:**
- Decisions affecting rights or entitlements
- Decisions with significant consequences
- Decisions requiring human judgment
- Decisions affecting vulnerable groups

**Why Avoid:**
- Legal and ethical concerns
- Accountability challenges
- Risk of harm
- Lack of human oversight

**Evaluation Framework:**
- **Value:** What is the potential value?
- **Feasibility:** Is it technically feasible?
- **Risk:** What are the risks?
- **Appropriateness:** Is AI the right solution?

### 5.2 The "Build vs. Buy" Decision

**Working with Commercial Colleagues Early:**

**Why Early Engagement Matters:**
- Better understanding of options
- Informed decision-making
- Faster procurement
- Better outcomes

**Key Considerations:**

**Build:**
- **Control:** Full control over development
- **Customization:** Tailored to specific needs
- **Capability:** Builds internal capability
- **Cost:** May be more expensive
- **Time:** Longer development time

**Buy:**
- **Speed:** Faster deployment
- **Cost:** Lower initial cost
- **Expertise:** Vendor expertise
- **Risk:** Less control, vendor dependency
- **Customization:** May be limited

**Intellectual Property (IP) Rights:**
- Who owns the IP?
- Can you modify the system?
- Can you switch vendors?
- What are the licensing terms?

**Vendor Lock-In:**
- Difficulty switching vendors
- Dependency on single vendor
- Limited flexibility
- Higher long-term costs

**Mitigation:**
- Contractual protections
- Data portability
- Exit strategies
- Open standards
- Multi-vendor strategies

### 5.3 Routes to Market

**Crown Commercial Service (CCS) Frameworks:**

**Benefits:**
- Pre-approved vendors
- Streamlined procurement
- Framework agreements
- Access to innovation
- Reduced procurement time

**AI-Specific Frameworks:**
- AI and Machine Learning services
- Cloud services
- Data analytics
- Software development

**Dynamic Purchasing Systems (DPS):**
- Open to new suppliers
- Continuous competition
- Flexible and responsive
- Innovation-friendly

**Other Routes:**
- Direct awards (where appropriate)
- Open competition
- Innovation partnerships
- Research collaborations

**Procurement Best Practices:**
- Early market engagement
- Clear requirements
- Fair competition
- Value for money
- Risk management

### 5.4 The Multidisciplinary Team

**Roles Required for Success:**

**Data Scientists:**
- Data analysis and modeling
- Model development
- Performance evaluation
- Technical expertise

**Ethicists:**
- Ethical assessment
- Bias identification
- Fairness evaluation
- Ethical guidance

**User Researchers:**
- User needs understanding
- User testing
- Trust measurement
- User experience

**Software Engineers:**
- System development
- Integration
- Testing and deployment
- Maintenance

**Other Key Roles:**
- **Project Managers:** Coordination and delivery
- **Commercial Leads:** Procurement and contracts
- **Legal Advisors:** Legal compliance
- **Security Specialists:** Security and risk
- **Policy Advisors:** Policy alignment

**Team Composition:**
- Right mix of skills
- Clear roles and responsibilities
- Effective collaboration
- Shared understanding

**Challenges:**
- Finding right skills
- Team coordination
- Communication across disciplines
- Resource constraints

## Implementation Framework

**Project Lifecycle:**
1. **Discovery:** Identify use case and requirements
2. **Alpha:** Test feasibility and approach
3. **Beta:** Develop and test solution
4. **Live:** Deploy and operate
5. **Retirement:** Plan for decommissioning

**Governance:**
- Clear accountability
- Regular reviews
- Risk management
- Quality assurance

## Key Takeaways

1. **Start with use cases:** Identify high-value opportunities, avoid high-risk automation
2. **Engage commercial early:** Build vs. buy decisions require commercial input
3. **Use frameworks:** CCS frameworks streamline procurement
4. **Build the right team:** Multidisciplinary teams are essential for success

## Reflection Questions

1. How do you identify high-value AI use cases while avoiding inappropriate automation?
2. What factors influence the "build vs. buy" decision for AI systems?
3. How do you navigate CCS frameworks and other routes to market?
4. What roles are essential in a multidisciplinary AI team, and how do they work together?
5. How do you manage vendor relationships and avoid lock-in?
