# Module 1: Foundations of Artificial Intelligence

**Objective:** Define AI, understand its sub-fields, and demystify the terminology used in government policy.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the OECD definition of AI
- Distinguish between different fields of AI (ML, CV, NLP)
- Understand Generative AI and Large Language Models
- Introduction to Agentic AI systems

## Core Topics

### 1.1 Defining AI

**Moving Beyond Science Fiction:**

**OECD Definition:**
- Machine-based systems that infer how to generate outputs
- Outputs include: predictions, content, decisions
- Systems learn from data or experience
- Not just automation, but inference and adaptation

**Key Characteristics:**
- **Machine-based:** Automated systems, not human
- **Inference:** Drawing conclusions from data
- **Output Generation:** Producing predictions, content, or decisions
- **Adaptive:** Can improve or adjust based on experience

**Common Misconceptions:**
- AI is not general intelligence (AGI)
- AI is not sentient or conscious
- AI does not "think" like humans
- AI is pattern matching, not true understanding

**Government Policy Context:**
- UK government uses OECD definition
- Focus on practical, deployable systems
- Emphasis on safety and governance
- Avoids hype and science fiction

### 1.2 The Fields of AI

**Machine Learning (ML):**
- **Definition:** Learning from data to identify patterns
- **Process:** Training on data, identifying patterns, making predictions
- **Example:** Fraud detection in financial transactions
- **Characteristics:** Requires training data, improves with more data
- **Applications:** Classification, prediction, anomaly detection

**Computer Vision (CV):**
- **Definition:** Interpreting visual information
- **Process:** Analyzing images and video to extract information
- **Example:** Medical imaging analysis, object detection
- **Characteristics:** Processes pixels, recognizes patterns in visual data
- **Applications:** Medical diagnosis, surveillance, autonomous vehicles

**Natural Language Processing (NLP):**
- **Definition:** Processing human language
- **Process:** Understanding, interpreting, and generating text
- **Example:** Text summarization, translation, sentiment analysis
- **Characteristics:** Works with unstructured text data
- **Applications:** Chatbots, document analysis, language translation

**Key Distinctions:**
- **ML:** General pattern learning from data
- **CV:** Specific to visual information
- **NLP:** Specific to language and text
- All are subsets of AI, often used together

### 1.3 Generative AI

**Understanding Large Language Models (LLMs):**

**What are LLMs?**
- Large neural networks trained on vast text datasets
- Generate text by predicting next words
- Probabilistic models: predict most likely next token
- Examples: GPT-4, Claude, Gemini

**Probabilistic Nature:**
- Models assign probabilities to possible outputs
- Generate text by sampling from probability distributions
- Same input can produce different outputs
- Non-deterministic behavior

**How They Differ from Traditional "Narrow" AI:**

**Traditional Narrow AI:**
- Task-specific systems
- Predictable outputs
- Trained for specific purposes
- Limited to training domain

**Generative AI:**
- General-purpose capabilities
- Can adapt to many tasks
- Trained on broad data
- Versatile but less predictable

**Key Differences:**
- **Scope:** Narrow vs general-purpose
- **Predictability:** Deterministic vs probabilistic
- **Training:** Specific vs broad datasets
- **Risk:** Lower vs higher risk

**Capabilities:**
- Text generation
- Question answering
- Summarization
- Translation
- Code generation
- Creative content

**Limitations:**
- Hallucination (confabulation)
- Lack of true understanding
- Bias from training data
- No real-time learning
- Context window limitations

### 1.4 Agentic AI

**Introduction to Autonomous Systems:**

**Definition:**
- AI systems that can execute multi-step tasks
- Operate with minimal human intervention
- Can use tools and interact with systems
- Goal-oriented and autonomous

**Key Characteristics:**
- **Autonomy:** Can act independently
- **Multi-step:** Can plan and execute sequences
- **Tool Use:** Can interact with APIs, databases, systems
- **Goal-Oriented:** Works toward specific objectives

**Examples:**
- Autonomous agents that can research and write reports
- AI assistants that can book appointments and send emails
- Systems that can analyze data and make recommendations
- Agents that can interact with multiple systems

**Capabilities:**
- Planning and execution
- Tool and API usage
- Multi-system interaction
- Autonomous decision-making
- Task completion

**Risks:**
- Higher autonomy means higher risk
- Can take unintended actions
- Difficult to predict behavior
- Accountability challenges
- Safety concerns

**Governance Considerations:**
- Requires stronger oversight
- Need for safety mechanisms
- Human-in-the-loop requirements
- Clear boundaries and limits
- Monitoring and control

## Key Terminology

**AI (Artificial Intelligence):** Machine-based systems that infer how to generate outputs

**ML (Machine Learning):** Learning from data to identify patterns

**CV (Computer Vision):** Interpreting visual information

**NLP (Natural Language Processing):** Processing human language

**LLM (Large Language Model):** Large neural networks for text generation

**Generative AI:** AI that generates new content (text, images, etc.)

**Agentic AI:** Autonomous systems that execute multi-step tasks

## Key Takeaways

1. **AI is practical:** OECD definition focuses on deployable systems, not science fiction
2. **AI has many fields:** ML, CV, and NLP serve different purposes
3. **Generative AI is different:** Probabilistic and versatile, but higher risk
4. **Agentic AI requires care:** Higher autonomy means higher governance requirements
