# Module 7: Governance and Accountability

**Objective:** Establish the oversight structures required to run AI responsibly.

## Learning Objectives

- Establish AI Governance Boards and Ethics Committees
- Define Senior Responsible Owners (SROs) and accountability chains
- Maintain AI Systems Inventory for audit purposes
- Move from "paper coordination" to "system supervision"

## Core Topics

### 7.1 Governance Boards

**The Role of AI Governance Boards:**

**Purpose:**
- Strategic oversight of AI use
- Risk management and mitigation
- Ethical review and approval
- Policy and standards development

**Responsibilities:**
- **Strategic:** Setting AI strategy and direction
- **Oversight:** Monitoring AI deployments
- **Risk Management:** Identifying and managing risks
- **Ethics:** Ensuring ethical AI use
- **Compliance:** Ensuring regulatory compliance

**Composition:**
- Senior leadership
- Technical experts
- Ethics advisors
- Legal advisors
- User representatives

**Ethics Committees:**

**Purpose:**
- Ethical review of AI systems
- Bias and fairness assessment
- Impact evaluation
- Ethical guidance

**Responsibilities:**
- Review AI proposals
- Assess ethical implications
- Provide ethical guidance
- Monitor ethical compliance
- Escalate ethical concerns

**Integration:**
- Governance boards and ethics committees work together
- Clear roles and responsibilities
- Effective coordination
- Shared understanding

### 7.2 Accountability

**Defining "Senior Responsible Owners" (SROs):**

**Role:**
- Ultimate accountability for AI systems
- Strategic oversight
- Risk ownership
- Decision-making authority

**Responsibilities:**
- **Strategic:** Setting direction and vision
- **Governance:** Ensuring appropriate governance
- **Risk:** Owning and managing risks
- **Accountability:** Answerable for outcomes
- **Resources:** Ensuring adequate resources

**Liability Chains:**

**Question:** Who is accountable when AI makes decisions?

**Potential Accountable Parties:**
- **Ministers:** Political accountability
- **Department Heads:** Administrative accountability
- **SROs:** Operational accountability
- **System Owners:** Technical accountability
- **Vendors:** Contractual accountability

**Mapping Accountability:**
- Clear accountability chains
- Defined roles and responsibilities
- Documented decision-making
- Transparent accountability

**From "Paper Coordination" to "System Supervision":**

**Traditional "Paper Coordination":**
- Paper-based processes
- Manual coordination
- Limited oversight
- Reactive management

**Modern "System Supervision":**
- Digital oversight systems
- Automated monitoring
- Real-time supervision
- Proactive management

**Transformation:**
- Digital governance tools
- Automated monitoring
- Real-time dashboards
- Proactive alerts
- Continuous oversight

### 7.3 AI Systems Inventory

**The Requirement to Maintain a Live Inventory:**

**Purpose:**
- Track all deployed AI systems
- Enable audit and oversight
- Support risk management
- Ensure compliance

**Inventory Contents:**
- System name and description
- Purpose and use case
- Technical details
- Data sources
- Risk assessment
- Governance arrangements
- Contact information

**Maintenance:**
- Keep inventory up to date
- Regular reviews
- Add new systems
- Remove retired systems
- Update changes

**Benefits:**
- Visibility of AI use
- Audit trail
- Risk management
- Compliance support
- Knowledge management

**Challenges:**
- Keeping inventory current
- Resource requirements
- System identification
- Change management

**Implementation:**
- Centralized inventory system
- Clear processes
- Regular updates
- Access controls
- Reporting capabilities

## Governance Framework

**Structure:**
- Governance boards
- Ethics committees
- SROs
- System owners
- Technical teams

**Processes:**
- Approval processes
- Review processes
- Escalation processes
- Monitoring processes
- Reporting processes

**Tools:**
- Inventory systems
- Monitoring tools
- Reporting dashboards
- Risk registers
- Compliance tracking

## Key Takeaways

1. **Governance boards are essential:** Strategic oversight and risk management
2. **SROs provide accountability:** Clear ownership and responsibility
3. **Inventory enables oversight:** Track and monitor all AI systems
4. **System supervision is modern:** Digital oversight replaces paper coordination

4. How does the shift from "paper coordination" to "system supervision" change governance?
5. How do governance structures ensure responsible AI use while enabling innovation?
