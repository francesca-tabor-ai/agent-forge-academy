# Module 6: Economic Security & Geopolitics

**Focus:** Navigating global supply chains and protecting national resilience.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand trade policy shift from passive openness to strategic industry building
- Analyze supply chain resilience and dependency mapping
- Evaluate the sovereign AI debate and balancing collaboration with control
- Recognize investment screening's role in protecting sensitive technologies

## Core Topics

### Trade Policy in the AI Age

**Moving from Passive to Strategic Trade:**

**Passive Trade (Old Model):**
- Openness for its own sake
- Market-led development
- Neutral approach
- Reactive policy

**Strategic Industry Building (New Model):**
- Trade reinforces domestic capability
- Strategic sector focus
- Active policy
- Proactive development

**Key Principles:**
- Trade supports industrial strategy
- Strategic sectors prioritized
- Capability building focus
- Economic security considered

**Mechanisms:**
- Strategic trade agreements
- Sector-specific support
- Export promotion
- Import management

**Benefits:**
- Builds domestic capability
- Reduces dependence
- Creates competitive advantage
- Enhances security

### Supply Chain Resilience

**Reducing Exposure to External Shocks:**

**The Problem:**
- Global supply chains
- Dependencies on others
- Vulnerability to shocks
- Risk of coercion

**Critical Inputs:**
- **Semiconductors:** Essential for AI
- **Critical Minerals:** Rare earths, lithium
- **Components:** Advanced materials
- **Infrastructure:** Data centers, networks

**Dependency Mapping:**
- Identify critical dependencies
- Assess vulnerability
- Map supply chains
- Identify alternatives

**Resilience Strategies:**
- Diversification
- Domestic capacity
- Strategic stockpiling
- Alternative suppliers

**Benefits:**
- Reduced vulnerability
- Economic security
- Strategic autonomy
- Crisis resilience

### The "Sovereign AI" Debate

**Balancing Collaboration with Control:**

**The Tension:**
- International collaboration needed
- Domestic control required
- Open research vs. security
- Innovation vs. protection

**Sovereign AI Arguments:**
- National security
- Economic sovereignty
- Strategic autonomy
- Reduced dependence

**Collaboration Arguments:**
- Innovation benefits
- Cost efficiency
- Global standards
- Research excellence

**Balancing Approach:**
- Strategic sectors: Sovereign control
- Research: International collaboration
- Infrastructure: Domestic capacity
- Standards: Global alignment

**Key Questions:**
- What must be sovereign?
- What can be collaborative?
- Where are the boundaries?
- How to manage risks?

### Investment Screening

**Protecting Sensitive Technologies:**

**National Security and Investment Act (NSIA):**
- Screening foreign investment
- Protecting sensitive sectors
- National security focus
- Strategic assets

**Sensitive Technologies:**
- AI and machine learning
- Semiconductors
- Quantum computing
- Advanced materials

**Screening Criteria:**
- National security risk
- Strategic importance
- Foreign control
- Technology transfer

**Challenges:**
- Balancing openness and security
- Defining sensitive sectors
- Avoiding overreach
- Maintaining investment attractiveness

**Benefits:**
- Protects strategic assets
- Prevents technology leakage
- Maintains security
- Preserves capability

**Open Economy:**
- Not protectionist
- Selective screening
- Transparent process
- Investment-friendly

## Key Takeaways

1. **Strategic trade:** Move from passive openness to trade policy that reinforces domestic capability
2. **Supply chain resilience:** Map dependencies in semiconductors and critical minerals, reduce vulnerability
3. **Sovereign AI:** Balance international collaboration with domestic control over critical technologies
4. **Investment screening:** NSIA protects sensitive technologies while maintaining open economy

## Reflection Questions

1. How should trade policy shift from passive openness to strategic industry building?
2. How do you map supply chain dependencies and build resilience in critical inputs?
3. How do you balance sovereign AI control with international collaboration?
4. How can investment screening protect sensitive technologies without harming investment?
5. What is the relationship between economic security and industrial strategy in the AI era?
