# Governing the Productive State: Strategy and Industrial Policy in the AI Era

## Course Overview

This course examines the shift from "market stewardship" to the "Productive State" necessitated by the rise of Artificial Intelligence. It explores why AI must be treated as an industrial infrastructure rather than merely a digital service, and how the state must align trade, finance, energy, and skills to build sovereign capability and economic resilience.

## Course Description

This course examines the shift from "market stewardship" to the "Productive State" necessitated by the rise of Artificial Intelligence. It explores why AI must be treated as an industrial infrastructure rather than merely a digital service, and how the state must align trade, finance, energy, and skills to build sovereign capability and economic resilience.

The course covers theoretical foundations, industrial applications across eight priority sectors, the four pillars of production (compute, data, talent, energy), institutional architecture, finance and scaling, economic security, regional strategy, and regulatory approaches.

## Course Structure

### Module 1: The End of Laissez-Faire & The Return of Strategy
**Focus:** Understanding the theoretical shift in economic governance caused by AI.

**Key Topics:**
- The Structural Shock of AI: General-purpose industrial technology
- The Limits of Market Stewardship: Why neutral arbiter model fails
- Defining the "Productive State": Governing for production
- The "AI Maker vs. AI Taker" Dilemma: Strategic choice

### Module 2: AI as Industrial Infrastructure (The "IS-8" Sectors)
**Focus:** Applying AI across the eight growth-driving sectors identified in the modern Industrial Strategy.

**Key Topics:**
- Beyond Chatbots: Industrial applications in manufacturing, logistics, energy
- Sector Deep Dive: Advanced Manufacturing, Clean Energy, Creative Industries, Defence, Digital and Technologies, Financial Services, Life Sciences, Professional Business Services
- Advanced Manufacturing & Robotics: Digital twins and sovereign production capacity
- Foundational Industries: Steel, chemicals, and ports as critical inputs

### Module 3: The Four Pillars of Production (Inputs & Constraints)
**Focus:** The physical and human capital requirements for AI scalability.

**Key Topics:**
- The Coordination Problem: Markets fail to align compute, data, talent, energy
- Energy as the Binding Constraint: High prices and grid delays
- Sovereign Compute & Infrastructure: 20x increase in public compute capacity
- Data as an Asset: National Data Library and NHS data as strategic assets

### Module 4: Institutional Architecture & The Department for Business and Trade (DBT)
**Focus:** Reforming government institutions to deliver industrial strategy.

**Key Topics:**
- Rebuilding the DBT: From trade facilitator to institutional architect
- Mission-Led Governance: Industrial Strategy Council on statutory footing
- Aligning Levers: Tax, regulation, and procurement coordination
- Public Procurement as Industrial Policy: £400bn+ purchasing power

### Module 5: Finance, Investment, and Scaling
**Focus:** Solving the "scale-up" problem and mobilizing capital.

**Key Topics:**
- The "Scale-Up" Gap: Why UK excels at startups but fails at scaling
- Patient Capital & Pension Reform: Mansion House reforms unlock long-term investment
- State Finance Institutions: National Wealth Fund, British Business Bank, UK Export Finance
- Export Finance as Strategy: Preferential support for strategic sectors

### Module 6: Economic Security & Geopolitics
**Focus:** Navigating global supply chains and protecting national resilience.

**Key Topics:**
- Trade Policy in the AI Age: From passive openness to strategic industry building
- Supply Chain Resilience: Mapping dependencies in semiconductors and critical minerals
- The "Sovereign AI" Debate: Balancing collaboration with control
- Investment Screening: NSIA protecting sensitive technologies

### Module 7: Regional Strategy & Place-Based Growth
**Focus:** Ensuring AI benefits are distributed beyond London and the South East.

**Key Topics:**
- AI Growth Zones: North/South Wales, Oxford-Cambridge arc with planning reform
- Clusters and Devolution: Mayors and Combined Authorities tailoring strategy
- Bridging the Gap: Connecting R&D powerhouses (universities) with industrial bases (North/Midlands)

### Module 8: The Regulatory Battlefield
**Focus:** Balancing safety, ethics, and innovation.

**Key Topics:**
- Pro-Innovation Regulation: UK's Goldilocks approach (application not technology)
- Regulatory Innovation Office (RIO): Reducing time to market
- Intellectual Property & Copyright: Balancing AI training needs with creative industries rights
- Labor Market Transition: Right to switch off, surveillance protections, augmentation focus

## Learning Outcomes

Upon completion of this course, participants will be able to:

1. Understand why AI requires a shift from market stewardship to productive state model
2. Analyze AI applications across the IS-8 priority sectors and foundational industries
3. Recognize the four pillars of production (compute, data, talent, energy) and coordination needs
4. Evaluate institutional architecture reforms needed for industrial strategy delivery
5. Assess financial architecture for scaling AI industrialization
6. Navigate economic security and geopolitical challenges in AI supply chains
7. Design regional strategies for place-based AI growth
8. Balance pro-innovation regulation with safety and worker protections
