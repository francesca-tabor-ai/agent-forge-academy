# Module 4: Institutional Architecture & The Department for Business and Trade (DBT)

**Focus:** Reforming government institutions to deliver industrial strategy.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand DBT's transformation from trade facilitator to institutional architect
- Recognize the importance of mission-led governance and durable institutions
- Analyze how to align tax, regulation, and procurement levers
- Evaluate public procurement as industrial policy tool

## Core Topics

### Rebuilding the DBT

**Transforming from Trade Facilitator to Institutional Architect:**

**Traditional DBT Role:**
- Trade facilitation
- Export promotion
- Market access
- Trade negotiations

**New Role: Institutional Architect:**
- Design productive state
- Coordinate industrial strategy
- Build institutional capacity
- Strategic planning

**Key Functions:**
- Industrial strategy development
- Sector coordination
- Capability building
- Strategic investment

**Why This Matters:**
- Central coordination needed
- Strategic direction required
- Institutional capacity essential
- Long-term planning critical

### Mission-Led Governance

**Establishing Durable Institutions:**

**The Problem:**
- Political cycles disrupt strategy
- Institutions created and abolished
- Lack of continuity
- Lost expertise

**Solution: Durable Institutions:**

**Industrial Strategy Council:**
- Statutory footing
- Independent oversight
- Long-term perspective
- Survives political change

**Key Features:**
- **Independence:** Not subject to political whim
- **Expertise:** Technical and strategic knowledge
- **Continuity:** Endures across governments
- **Accountability:** Clear mandate and reporting

**Benefits:**
- Strategic continuity
- Institutional memory
- Expert advice
- Long-term planning

**Other Durable Institutions:**
- Sector councils
- Strategic bodies
- Advisory groups
- Implementation agencies

### Aligning Levers

**Coordinating Tax, Regulation, and Procurement:**

**The Challenge:**
- Multiple policy levers
- Often uncoordinated
- Contradictory signals
- Inefficient outcomes

**Tax Policy:**
- R&D tax credits
- Investment incentives
- Sector-specific support
- Strategic alignment

**Regulation:**
- Pro-innovation approach
- Sector-specific rules
- Standards setting
- Market shaping

**Procurement:**
- Strategic purchasing
- Innovation support
- Domestic preference
- Capability building

**Coordination:**
- Aligned signals
- Consistent policy
- Strategic direction
- Maximum impact

### Public Procurement as Industrial Policy

**Using £400bn+ Purchasing Power:**

**Scale of Public Procurement:**
- £400bn+ annual spending
- Largest buyer in economy
- Shapes markets
- Creates demand

**Strategic Use:**
- Create demand for innovation
- Anchor domestic production
- Support supply chains
- Build capabilities

**Key Mechanisms:**
- Innovation procurement
- Strategic partnerships
- Long-term contracts
- Capability requirements

**Examples:**
- Defence procurement
- Infrastructure projects
- Digital services
- Healthcare systems

**Benefits:**
- Market creation
- Innovation support
- Domestic anchoring
- Strategic capability

**Challenges:**
- Balancing value and strategy
- EU procurement rules
- Competition requirements
- Efficiency concerns

## Key Takeaways

1. **DBT transformation:** From trade facilitator to institutional architect of productive state
2. **Durable institutions:** Industrial Strategy Council on statutory footing survives political cycles
3. **Aligned levers:** Tax, regulation, and procurement coordinated for strategic impact
4. **Procurement power:** £400bn+ public purchasing creates demand and anchors domestic production

## Reflection Questions

1. How should DBT transform from trade facilitator to institutional architect?
2. Why are durable institutions like the Industrial Strategy Council necessary, and how do they survive political cycles?
3. How can tax, regulation, and procurement be aligned to support industrial strategy?
4. How can public procurement be used strategically while maintaining value and competition?
5. What institutional reforms are needed to deliver effective industrial strategy?
