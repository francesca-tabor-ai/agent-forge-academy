# Governing the Future: AI, State Capacity, and the Built Environment

## Course Overview

This course explores how AI is transforming the British state from an "analogue" bureaucracy into a digital systems integrator, with a specific focus on the planning system, housing delivery, and the ethical implications of automated governance.

## Course Description

This course examines the role of Artificial Intelligence in modernizing state capacity, using the UK's planning and security systems as primary case studies. Students will analyze the shift from discretionary, paper-based governance to data-driven, rules-based systems. The course covers technical innovations (such as the "Extract" tool), political economy debates (NIMBYism vs. YIMBYism), and the civil liberty risks associated with the "Security State."

## Course Structure

### Module 1: The Crisis of the Analog State
**Focus:** Understanding the structural failures of the current system that necessitate AI intervention.

**Key Topics:**
- The Information Problem: Planning system as failed information system
- The Productivity Paradox: Uncertainty discourages innovation
- The Housing Crisis Context: Supply-demand imbalance and land constraints
- Case Study: Mountains of paper maps and legacy microfiche

### Module 2: Digitizing the Past – The "Extract" Tool
**Focus:** The technical and practical application of AI in unlocking historical data.

**Key Topics:**
- The "Extract" Initiative: MHCLG and i.AI collaboration with DeepMind
- Technical Workflow: LLMs, VLMs, and SAM for document digitization
- Performance & Metrics: 1-2 hours to 3 minutes, 100% text, 94% dates
- Evaluation-Driven Development: Ground truth testing before rollout

### Module 3: From Discretion to Rules – Digital Zoning
**Focus:** The theoretical shift in how territory is governed.

**Key Topics:**
- The Role of the State: DLUHC as digital systems integrator
- Machine-Readable Plans: PDF to standardized data models
- Upstream Democracy: Shifting contestation to plan-making stage
- Infrastructure Alignment: ALIGN spatial tool for coordination

### Module 4: The AI Arms Race – NIMBYs, YIMBYs, and Civic Engagement
**Focus:** How AI is being weaponized by opposing sides of the development debate.

**Key Topics:**
- Weaponized Objection: Objector.ai and AI-generated objections
- The Risk of Hallucination: False case law citations
- Digital Democracy: Consult AI system for processing responses
- PropTech for Engagement: 3D visualization and VR for silent majorities

### Module 5: The Security State and Civil Liberties
**Focus:** The ethical risks of AI when applied to internal security and borders.

**Key Topics:**
- The "New Security State": Predictive governance and risk scoring
- Algorithmic Bias: Automation bias and error propagation (Windrush precedents)
- Digital Borders: Automated status checks and eVisas
- Transparency vs. Efficiency: Black box algorithms vs. explainable decisions

### Module 6: Rebuilding State Capacity and Implementation
**Focus:** The practicalities of rolling out AI in government.

**Key Topics:**
- Procurement Reform: Digital Planning Directory and PropTech Innovation Fund
- Cultural Change: Skills gap and institutional stability
- Standardization: Planning Data Platform and open standards
- Future Outlook: 2026 digitization target and 1.5 million homes goal

## Course Metadata

- **Category:** Public Sector Governance
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Duration:** ~8-10 weeks
- **Best For:** Planning professionals, housing policy advisors, urban planners, local government officials, civil liberties advocates, security policy analysts
- **Industries:** Public Sector, Government, Urban Planning, Housing, Legal & Compliance

## Learning Outcomes

Upon completion of this course, participants will be able to:

1. Understand the structural failures of analog planning systems that necessitate AI
2. Analyze technical innovations like the Extract tool for digitizing historical data
3. Evaluate the shift from discretionary to rules-based governance (digital zoning)
4. Assess the AI "arms race" in civic engagement and its democratic implications
5. Recognize civil liberty risks in the Security State and digital borders
6. Implement practical strategies for rebuilding state capacity in planning and housing

- Reflection questions
- Implementation guidance
