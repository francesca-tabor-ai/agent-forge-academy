# Module 3: From Discretion to Rules – Digital Zoning

**Focus:** The theoretical shift in how territory is governed.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the shift from policy department to digital systems integrator
- Recognize the transition to machine-readable plans
- Explore upstream democracy concept
- Analyze infrastructure alignment tools

## Core Topics

### The Role of the State

**Moving DLUHC from Policy Department to Digital Systems Integrator:**

**Traditional Role:**
- Policy development
- Regulation and guidance
- Coordination and oversight
- Limited technical involvement

**Digital Systems Integrator:**
- Design and operate systems
- Build technical infrastructure
- Integrate digital platforms
- Direct technical capability

**Key Shift:**
- **From:** Policy coordination
- **To:** System design and operation
- **From:** Guidance and regulation
- **To:** Technical infrastructure
- **From:** External coordination
- **To:** Direct capability

**Why It Matters:**
- AI requires system-level thinking
- Technical infrastructure needed
- Integration across systems
- Strategic direction required

### Machine-Readable Plans

**The Transition from PDF-Based to Standardized Data Models:**

**Current State:**
- Plans in PDF format
- Human-readable only
- Cannot be processed automatically
- Difficult to check compliance

**Machine-Readable Plans:**
- Standardized data models
- Structured formats
- Automated processing
- Compliance checking

**Benefits:**
- Automated compliance checking
- Faster processing
- Consistent application
- Reduced errors

**Standardization:**
- Common data models
- Interoperable formats
- Shared standards
- Integration enabled

**Implementation:**
- Define data models
- Convert existing plans
- Create new plans in standard format
- Enable automated processing

### Upstream Democracy

**Shifting Democratic Contestation to Plan-Making Stage:**

**Current Model:**
- **Individual Application Stage:** Discretionary decisions
- **Democratic Contestation:** At application level
- **Uncertainty:** Outcomes unpredictable
- **Delay:** Long decision processes

**Proposed Model:**
- **Plan-Making Stage:** Rules established democratically
- **Application Stage:** Rules applied automatically
- **Certainty:** Predictable outcomes
- **Speed:** Faster decisions

**Benefits:**
- **Certainty:** Predictable outcomes
- **Speed:** Faster processing
- **Democracy:** Upstream, not downstream
- **Efficiency:** Reduced delays

**Democratic Process:**
- Public engagement in plan-making
- Democratic decisions on rules
- Rules applied consistently
- Reduced individual discretion

**Trade-offs:**
- Less discretion at application stage
- More democracy at plan stage
- Predictability vs. flexibility
- Rules vs. judgment

## Governance Transformation

**From Discretion to Rules:**
- **Discretionary:** Case-by-case decisions
- **Rules-Based:** Automated application
- **Upstream Democracy:** Rules set democratically
- **Downstream Automation:** Rules applied automatically

**System Integration:**
- Digital platforms
- Integrated systems
- Coordinated planning
- Strategic direction

## Key Takeaways

1. **State as integrator:** DLUHC becomes digital systems integrator, not just policy department
2. **Machine-readable plans:** Standardized data models enable automated compliance checking
3. **Upstream democracy:** Democratic contestation shifts to plan-making, not individual applications
4. **Infrastructure alignment:** Tools like ALIGN coordinate infrastructure and housing spatially
