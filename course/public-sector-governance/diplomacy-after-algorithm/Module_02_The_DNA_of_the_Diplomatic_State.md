# Module 2: The DNA of the Diplomatic State

**Theme:** Historical context on the institutional culture that AI is disrupting.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the clash between Colonial Office and Foreign Office traditions
- Recognize the role of tacit vs. explicit knowledge in British diplomacy
- Analyze the 2020 FCDO merger and its consequences
- Evaluate how institutional culture affects AI adoption

## Core Topics

### Two Traditions

**The Clash Between Colonial Office and Foreign Office:**

**Colonial Office Legacy:**
- Managing difference
- Tacit knowledge
- Administrative focus
- Practical expertise

**Foreign Office Elite Culture:**
- Relationship-based
- Generalist approach
- Negotiation focus
- Diplomatic networks

**The Clash:**
- Different values
- Competing priorities
- Cultural conflict
- Integration challenges

**Why This Matters:**
- Affects AI adoption
- Shapes institutional response
- Influences capability building
- Determines effectiveness

### Tacit vs. Explicit Knowledge

**How British Diplomacy Relied on Tacit Expertise:**

**Tacit Knowledge:**
- Informal networks
- Unwritten rules
- Context-dependent
- Experience-based

**Explicit Knowledge:**
- Structured data
- Documented processes
- Formal systems
- Codified information

**The Challenge:**
- Tacit knowledge difficult to translate
- AI requires structured data
- Informal networks not captured
- Experience hard to systematize

**Translation Problems:**
- Context lost in codification
- Relationships not captured
- Nuance disappears
- Judgment not replicable

**Why This Is Critical:**
- AI needs explicit knowledge
- Tacit knowledge valuable but inaccessible
- Balance required
- Hybrid approaches needed

### The 2020 Merger (FCDO)

**Case Study: Creation of the Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office:**

**The Merger:**
- Hasty combination
- Diplomacy and development
- Political decision
- Rapid implementation

**Culture Clashes:**
- Different traditions
- Competing priorities
- Value conflicts
- Integration failures

**Loss of Specialist Knowledge:**
- Development expertise marginalized
- Regional knowledge lost
- Technical capability reduced
- Evidence-based policy weakened

**Marginalization of Development Policy:**
- Aid policy deprioritized
- Long-term thinking lost
- Soft power reduced
- Strategic influence diminished

**Consequences:**
- Reduced capability
- Fragmented expertise
- Weakened influence
- Strategic vulnerability

**Lessons for AI:**
- Mergers need careful planning
- Expertise must be preserved
- Culture matters
- Capability building essential

## Key Takeaways

1. **Two traditions:** Colonial Office (tacit, administrative) vs. Foreign Office (relationship-based, generalist) create cultural clash
2. **Tacit knowledge:** British diplomacy's reliance on informal networks and tacit expertise is difficult to translate for AI
3. **FCDO merger:** Hasty 2020 merger led to culture clashes, loss of specialist knowledge, and marginalization of development policy
4. **Institutional culture:** Shapes AI adoption and determines whether AI enhances or undermines diplomatic capability

## Reflection Questions

1. How do the Colonial Office and Foreign Office traditions differ, and why does this matter for AI adoption?
2. Why is tacit knowledge difficult to translate into the structured datasets required for AI systems?
3. What were the consequences of the hasty FCDO merger for diplomatic capability and development policy?
4. How does institutional culture affect the state's ability to adopt AI effectively?
5. What lessons does the FCDO merger offer for rebuilding diplomatic capability with AI?
