# Module 5: Rebuilding the Institution – The AI-Capable Foreign Office

**Theme:** Structural reforms required to govern AI effectively.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand data as strategic infrastructure requiring ownership and standardization
- Recognize the necessity of rebuilding in-house capability
- Analyze the importance of hybrid teams breaking down silos
- Evaluate how to maintain strategic autonomy in AI adoption

## Core Topics

### Data as Strategic Infrastructure

**Shifting from Administrative By-Product to Public Asset:**

**Current State:**
- Data as by-product
- Administrative function
- Not strategic asset
- Underutilized

**New Approach:**
- Data as public asset
- Strategic infrastructure
- Owned and maintained
- Ensures sovereignty

**Ownership:**
- State controls data
- Public asset
- Strategic resource
- Sovereignty maintained

**Standardization:**
- Common formats
- Interoperable systems
- Shared standards
- Integration enabled

**Maintenance:**
- Quality assurance
- Regular updates
- Security protection
- Strategic management

**Why This Matters:**
- Enables AI capability
- Maintains sovereignty
- Supports strategic autonomy
- Creates competitive advantage

### Restoring Expertise

**The Necessity of Rebuilding In-House Capability:**

**Current Problem:**
- Outsourced analysis
- External dependency
- Lost capability
- Strategic vulnerability

**Why State Must Be Producer:**
- Strategic autonomy
- Independent analysis
- Reduced dependency
- Enhanced capability

**Not Just Consumer:**
- Cannot rely on vendors
- Must understand systems
- Need analytical capability
- Require technical expertise

**Rebuilding Capability:**
- In-house expertise
- Technical skills
- Analytical capacity
- Strategic thinking

**Benefits:**
- Strategic autonomy
- Better decisions
- Reduced dependency
- Enhanced effectiveness

### Hybrid Teams

**Breaking Down Silos Through Collaboration:**

**The Problem:**
- Silos between functions
- Technologists isolated
- Regional experts separate
- Diplomats disconnected

**Hybrid Teams:**
- Technologists
- Regional experts
- Diplomats
- Working together

**Breaking Down Silos:**
- Cross-functional teams
- Shared objectives
- Integrated approaches
- Collaborative culture

**Challenging Model Assumptions:**
- Question AI outputs
- Test assumptions
- Validate results
- Ensure accuracy

**Benefits:**
- Better outcomes
- Reduced errors
- Enhanced understanding
- Improved decisions

**Key Principles:**
- Collaboration
- Integration
- Challenge
- Validation

## Key Takeaways

1. **Data as infrastructure:** Shift from administrative by-product to strategic public asset that must be owned, standardized, and maintained
2. **Restoring expertise:** State must be producer of analysis, not just consumer, to maintain strategic autonomy
3. **Hybrid teams:** Break down silos by creating teams where technologists, regional experts, and diplomats work together
4. **Challenge assumptions:** Hybrid teams must challenge model assumptions to ensure accuracy and relevance

## Reflection Questions

1. How should data be treated as strategic infrastructure rather than administrative by-product?
2. Why must the state be a producer of analysis rather than just a consumer?
3. How do hybrid teams break down silos and improve AI-assisted diplomacy?
4. Why is it important for hybrid teams to challenge model assumptions?
5. What structural reforms are needed to build an AI-capable Foreign Office?
