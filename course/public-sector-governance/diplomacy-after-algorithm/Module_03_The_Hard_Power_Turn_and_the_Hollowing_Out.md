# Module 3: The Hard Power Turn and the Hollowing Out

**Theme:** The political and industrial context in which AI is being adopted.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the security-first framework and its impact on foreign policy
- Recognize the outsourcing trap and techno-bureaucratic capture
- Analyze how politicization of aid erodes soft power
- Evaluate the hollowing out of state capability

## Core Topics

### The Security-First Framework

**How Foreign Policy Has Been Subsumed by Defense:**

**Security Priorities:**
- Defense and security dominate
- Threat detection focus
- Narrow strategic imagination
- Reactive approach

**Narrowing of Strategic Imagination:**
- Problems viewed through security lens
- Threat models favored
- Opportunities missed
- Soft power neglected

**AI Security Tools:**
- Designed for threat detection
- Pattern recognition for risks
- Surveillance capabilities
- Defense applications

**Impact on Foreign Policy:**
- Less diplomatic engagement
- Reduced soft power
- Narrower strategic options
- Reactive rather than proactive

**Why This Matters:**
- Limits strategic options
- Reduces influence
- Undermines relationships
- Weakens long-term position

### The Outsourcing Trap

**Risks of Techno-Bureaucratic Capture:**

**External Consultants and Contractors:**
- Private firms provide analysis
- Strategic dependency created
- Capability hollowed out
- State becomes consumer

**Strategic Dependency:**
- Cannot act independently
- Relies on external expertise
- Loses analytical capability
- Reduced strategic autonomy

**Hollowing Out:**
- In-house capability lost
- Cannot be "intelligent client"
- Dependent on vendors
- Strategic vulnerability

**Techno-Bureaucratic Capture:**
- Vendors shape thinking
- Tools determine approach
- Commercial interests influence
- State capacity eroded

**Why This Is Dangerous:**
- Loss of strategic autonomy
- Reduced capability
- Dependency on others
- Vulnerability to capture

### Politicisation of Aid

**How Short-Term Incentives Erode Long-Term Influence:**

**Short-Term Political Incentives:**
- Immediate results sought
- Political credit prioritized
- Long-term thinking lost
- Strategic patience absent

**Erosion of Reputational Capital:**
- Trust undermined
- Relationships damaged
- Influence reduced
- Soft power weakened

**Making State Less Capable:**
- Cannot sustain trust
- Lacks long-term commitment
- Inconsistent approach
- Reduced effectiveness

**Sustained Trust Required:**
- Soft power needs consistency
- Relationships take time
- Reputation matters
- Influence is earned

**Impact:**
- Reduced soft power
- Weakened influence
- Less effective diplomacy
- Strategic disadvantage

## Key Takeaways

1. **Security-first framework:** Foreign policy subsumed by defense priorities, narrowing strategic imagination
2. **Outsourcing trap:** Reliance on external consultants creates strategic dependency and hollows out state capability
3. **Techno-bureaucratic capture:** Vendors shape thinking and tools, reducing state's ability to be intelligent client
4. **Politicization of aid:** Short-term incentives erode long-term influence and soft power, making state less capable

## Reflection Questions

1. How does the security-first framework narrow strategic imagination and limit foreign policy options?
2. What are the risks of outsourcing analysis to external consultants and contractors?
3. How does techno-bureaucratic capture undermine the state's ability to act as an intelligent client?
4. How does the politicization of aid erode long-term influence and soft power?
5. What does the hollowing out of state capability mean for diplomatic effectiveness in the AI era?
