# Diplomacy After the Algorithm: Governing Foreign Policy in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

## Course Overview

This course explores the structural transformation of the British Diplomatic State as it confronts the "algorithmic world order". It argues that AI is not merely a technical tool but a "structural force" that exposes existing institutional weaknesses, such as the erosion of expertise and the fragmentation of data. Students will examine the tension between traditional "human-centred diplomacy" and emerging "data-driven geopolitics," focusing on how institutions must be rebuilt to govern complexity without surrendering democratic accountability.

## Course Description

This course explores the structural transformation of the British Diplomatic State as it confronts the "algorithmic world order". It argues that AI is not merely a technical tool but a "structural force" that exposes existing institutional weaknesses, such as the erosion of expertise and the fragmentation of data.

The course examines the tension between traditional "human-centred diplomacy" and emerging "data-driven geopolitics," focusing on how institutions must be rebuilt to govern complexity without surrendering democratic accountability. It covers the crisis of the analogue state, the DNA of the diplomatic state, the hard power turn and hollowing out, data-driven geopolitics, rebuilding the institution, and governance and ethics.

## Course Structure

### Module 1: The Crisis of the Analogue State
**Theme:** Understanding why traditional diplomatic structures are failing to process modern geopolitical complexity.

**Key Topics:**
- Foreign Policy as a Data Problem: Volume, velocity, and complexity exceed human capacity
- The Limits of Human-Centred Diplomacy: Cognitive and organizational limitations, high-dimensional systems
- The Shift from Scarcity to Excess: Information overload drowning out strategic signals

### Module 2: The DNA of the Diplomatic State
**Theme:** Historical context on the institutional culture that AI is disrupting.

**Key Topics:**
- Two Traditions: Colonial Office (tacit, administrative) vs. Foreign Office (relationship-based, generalist)
- Tacit vs. Explicit Knowledge: British diplomacy's reliance on informal networks difficult to translate for AI
- The 2020 Merger (FCDO): Hasty merger led to culture clashes, loss of specialist knowledge, marginalization of development policy

### Module 3: The Hard Power Turn and the Hollowing Out
**Theme:** The political and industrial context in which AI is being adopted.

**Key Topics:**
- The Security-First Framework: Foreign policy subsumed by defense priorities, narrowing strategic imagination
- The Outsourcing Trap: Techno-bureaucratic capture by external consultants, strategic dependency, hollowing out
- Politicisation of Aid: Short-term incentives erode long-term influence and soft power

### Module 4: From Intuition to Data-Driven Geopolitics
**Theme:** Practical applications of AI in statecraft.

**Key Topics:**
- AI as a Force Multiplier: See, anticipate, and coordinate (not replace)
- Strategic Forecasting & Pattern Recognition: Weak signals, influence mapping, complex interactions
- Decision-Support Systems: Augmented decision-making, making uncertainty visible, avoiding black box

### Module 5: Rebuilding the Institution – The AI-Capable Foreign Office
**Theme:** Structural reforms required to govern AI effectively.

**Key Topics:**
- Data as Strategic Infrastructure: From administrative by-product to public asset, ownership and standardization
- Restoring Expertise: State must be producer of analysis, not just consumer, to maintain strategic autonomy
- Hybrid Teams: Break down silos, technologists, regional experts, and diplomats working together

### Module 6: Governance, Ethics, and Democratic Control
**Theme:** The constitutional and ethical challenges of algorithmic diplomacy.

**Key Topics:**
- Retaining Human Judgment: AI informs rather than decides, preventing automation bias
- Transparency and Accountability: Oversight mechanisms for Parliament and public scrutiny
- Strategic Renewal vs. Managed Decline: Using AI as catalyst for renewal or coping mechanism for decline

## Learning Outcomes

Upon completion of this course, participants will be able to:

1. Understand why traditional diplomatic structures fail to process modern geopolitical complexity
2. Analyze the institutional culture and historical context that AI is disrupting
3. Recognize the political and industrial context affecting AI adoption in foreign policy
4. Evaluate practical applications of AI in statecraft and data-driven geopolitics
5. Design structural reforms to build an AI-capable Foreign Office
6. Navigate governance, ethics, and democratic control challenges in algorithmic diplomacy

## Course Philosophy

This course emphasizes:
- **Structural Analysis:** AI as structural force exposing institutional weaknesses
- **Institutional Reform:** Rebuilding capability while maintaining democratic accountability
- **Human-AI Collaboration:** Augmentation not replacement, human judgment essential
- **Strategic Renewal:** Using AI as catalyst for renewal rather than coping mechanism

## Key Themes

- **Crisis of Analogue State:** Information overload and complexity exceed human capacity
- **Institutional DNA:** Historical traditions and culture shape AI adoption
- **Hollowing Out:** Outsourcing and politicization erode state capability
- **Data-Driven Geopolitics:** AI as force multiplier for seeing, anticipating, coordinating
- **Rebuilding Institution:** Data as infrastructure, restoring expertise, hybrid teams
- **Democratic Control:** Retaining human judgment, transparency, accountability, strategic renewal
