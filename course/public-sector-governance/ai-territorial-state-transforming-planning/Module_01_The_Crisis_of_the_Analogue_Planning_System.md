# Module 1: The Crisis of the Analogue Planning System

**Learning Objective:** Understand the structural failures of the current British planning system and why it is fundamentally an "information problem".

## Learning Objectives

- Analyze the information deficit in planning systems
- Understand economic and social consequences of restrictive planning
- Recognize the difference between discretionary and codified systems
- Examine case studies of planning system failures

## Core Topics

### Topic A: The Information Deficit

**Analyzing Why Planning Systems Struggle:**

**Data Collection and Processing:**
- Planning system struggles to collect data
- Difficult to process information
- Leads to slow decision-making
- Opaque processes

**Manual Review of Paper Documents:**
- Reliance on paper-based processes
- Manual document review
- Time-consuming
- Error-prone

**Lack of Standardized Data:**
- Thousands of local authorities
- Each with different systems
- No standardized formats
- Data silos

**Impact:**
- Slow decision-making
- Inconsistent outcomes
- Difficult to access information
- Poor transparency

**Why It's an Information Problem:**
- Information not accessible
- Data not standardized
- Processing is manual
- Systems don't communicate

### Topic B: Economic and Social Consequences

**The Link Between Planning and Economic Performance:**

**Restrictive Planning and Housing Undersupply:**
- Planning restrictions limit supply
- Housing undersupply results
- High house prices
- Affordability crisis

**The "Productivity Paradox" in UK Construction:**
- UK construction productivity low
- Uncertainty in planning reduces productivity
- Discourages innovation
- Increases costs

**Discretionary vs. Codified Rules:**

**Discretionary System:**
- Case-by-case judgment
- Human discretion
- Unpredictable outcomes
- High uncertainty

**Codified Rules:**
- Clear, written rules
- Predictable outcomes
- Automated checking possible
- Lower uncertainty

**How Uncertainty Raises Capital Costs:**
- Unpredictable outcomes increase risk
- Higher risk requires higher returns
- Capital costs increase
- Investment discouraged

**Case Study: Land Price Volatility**

**The Problem:**
- Land prices volatile relative to house prices
- Uncertainty in planning creates volatility
- Speculation increases
- Prices driven by planning, not value

**Impact on Intergenerational Inequality:**
- Older generations benefit from land value
- Younger generations priced out
- Wealth inequality increases
- Social mobility reduced

**Root Causes:**
- Planning uncertainty
- Land supply constraints
- Speculative behavior
- Information asymmetry

## System Failure Analysis

**Key Failures:**
- **Information:** Data not accessible or standardized
- **Process:** Manual, slow, opaque
- **Outcomes:** Unpredictable, inconsistent
- **Economics:** High costs, low productivity

**Why AI Is Needed:**
- Address information problems
- Standardize data
- Automate processing
- Enable predictability

## Key Takeaways

1. **Information problem:** Planning system fails due to information deficit
2. **Economic impact:** Restrictive planning reduces productivity and increases costs
3. **Discretionary system:** Uncertainty in discretionary system raises capital costs
4. **Intergenerational inequality:** Land price volatility driven by planning uncertainty

## Reflection Questions

1. How does the information deficit in planning systems cause slow and opaque decision-making?
2. What is the link between restrictive planning, housing undersupply, and construction productivity?
3. How does uncertainty in discretionary planning systems raise capital costs?
4. How does land price volatility relative to house prices impact intergenerational inequality?
5. Why is the planning system fundamentally an "information problem"?
