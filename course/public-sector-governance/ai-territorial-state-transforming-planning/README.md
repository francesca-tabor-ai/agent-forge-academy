# AI and the Territorial State: Transforming the British Planning System

## Course Overview

This course explores the intersection of artificial intelligence, urban planning, and state capacity, focusing on how the UK government is transitioning from an analogue, discretionary planning system to a digital, data-driven model.

## Course Description

This course explores the intersection of artificial intelligence, urban planning, and state capacity, focusing on how the UK government is transitioning from an analogue, discretionary planning system to a digital, data-driven model. It examines how AI tools address the "information problem" of land use, housing delivery, and infrastructure planning.

## Course Structure

### Module 1: The Crisis of the Analogue Planning System
**Learning Objective:** Understand the structural failures of the current British planning system and why it is fundamentally an "information problem".

**Key Topics:**
- The Information Deficit: Manual review, lack of standardized data
- Economic and Social Consequences: Productivity paradox, discretionary vs. codified rules, land price volatility

### Module 2: AI for Data Liberation and Digitization
**Learning Objective:** Examine the technical capabilities of AI in converting historic, unstructured planning data into usable digital assets.

**Key Topics:**
- The "Extract" Tool: VLM and SAM for text and geospatial extraction, efficiency metrics
- Standardizing Data: Machine-readable plans, Planning Data platform

### Module 3: The "AI Arms Race": Civic Engagement vs. Obstruction
**Learning Objective:** Analyze how AI is being used by both proponents and opponents of development, and implications for democracy.

**Key Topics:**
- AI for Objection: Objector.ai, hallucination risks, overwhelming planning departments
- Enhancing Public Consultation: Greater Cambridge case, democratizing access vs. supercharging NIMBYism

### Module 4: Automating Governance: From Validation to Decision
**Learning Objective:** Explore how AI assists in the administrative workflow of planning, from validation to decision-making.

**Key Topics:**
- Automating Validation: Planning Portal, PlanX, Doncaster Council case
- The Future of Decision Making: AI-augmented decisions, near-instant determinations

### Module 5: The Innovation Ecosystem and State Capacity
**Learning Objective:** Evaluate the role of the private sector (PropTech) and the government's role as a "systems integrator."

**Key Topics:**
- The PropTech Sector: £72bn revenue by 2032, procurement barriers, ODP network
- Rebuilding State Capacity: DLUHC as systems integrator, innovation funds, Digital Planning Directory

### Module 6: Future Directions: Digital Zoning and the Territorial State
**Learning Objective:** Assess the long-term vision of a "Digital Zoning" system and its potential to resolve the UK's housing and infrastructure challenges.

**Key Topics:**
- Digital Zoning Systems: Discretionary to codified rules, machine-readable plans, predictive modeling
- Infrastructure and Coordination: ALIGN tool, coordinating hidden infrastructure

## Course Metadata

- **Category:** Public Sector Governance
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Duration:** ~8-10 weeks
- **Best For:** Planning professionals, urban planners, housing policy advisors, local government officials, PropTech professionals, infrastructure planners
- **Industries:** Public Sector, Government, Urban Planning, Housing, Infrastructure

## Learning Outcomes

Upon completion of this course, participants will be able to:

1. Understand why the planning system is fundamentally an information problem
2. Analyze how AI tools like Extract digitize historic planning data
3. Evaluate the AI "arms race" in civic engagement and its democratic implications
4. Assess AI automation in planning validation and decision-making
5. Recognize the role of PropTech and state as systems integrator
6. Evaluate the vision for digital zoning and coordinated infrastructure

## Assessment

Each module includes:
- Learning objectives and key topics
- Real-world case studies (Extract, ALIGN, Objector.ai, Greater Cambridge, Doncaster)
- Technical and policy analysis
- Reflection questions
- Implementation guidance

## Prerequisites

- Understanding of planning systems and urban planning
- Familiarity with government structures and processes
- Interest in housing policy and infrastructure planning
- Background in planning, policy, or technology helpful but not required

## Course Philosophy

This course emphasizes:
- **Information Systems:** Planning as information problem, not just regulation
- **Practical Tools:** Real AI tools and their applications
- **Democratic Values:** Balancing efficiency with democratic participation
- **State Capacity:** Rebuilding capability for digital governance

## Key Themes

- **Information Problem:** Planning system failures due to information deficit
- **Data Liberation:** AI unlocks historic data and standardizes information
- **Democratic Engagement:** AI's role in civic participation and its risks
- **Automation:** AI assists validation and decision-making processes
- **Innovation Ecosystem:** PropTech sector and state coordination
- **Digital Zoning:** Future vision of codified rules and coordinated infrastructure
