# Capstone Project: Build a Decision OS

**Duration:** Weeks 9-10 (or extended timeline)  
**Theme:** *End-to-end causal decision system*

**Project Overview:**
Students will build a complete Decision Operating System for a real business decision. This capstone integrates all course concepts: decision framing, experiment design, causal inference, result interpretation, guardrails, and rollout decisions.

---

## Capstone Objectives

By completing this capstone, you will demonstrate ability to:

1. **Frame Business Decisions**
   - Identify decisions requiring causal certainty
   - Define success and guardrail metrics
   - Assess decision stakes

2. **Design Causal Methods**
   - Choose appropriate method (A/B test, causal inference)
   - Design experiments with proper power
   - Plan for guardrails and safety

3. **Analyze Results**
   - Interpret noisy and ambiguous results
   - Handle uncertainty appropriately
   - Detect unintended consequences

4. **Apply Guardrails**
   - Monitor safety metrics
   - Detect problems early
   - Plan for rollback

5. **Make Rollout Decisions**
   - Translate results into clear recommendations
   - Communicate uncertainty effectively
   - Build organizational learning

---

## Project Structure

### Phase 1: Decision Framing (Week 1)

**Deliverable: Decision Framing Document**

**Components:**

1. **Business Context**
   - What decision are we making?
   - Why does it matter?
   - What's at stake?
   - Who are the stakeholders?

2. **Decision Classification**
   - Can we randomize? (Experimentable)
   - Can we find natural variation? (Quasi-experimentable)
   - Only observational? (Observational)
   - Justify classification

3. **Success Metrics**
   - Primary outcome(s)
   - Definition and calculation
   - Business alignment
   - Practical significance thresholds

4. **Guardrail Metrics**
   - Safety metrics to protect
   - Degradation thresholds
   - Detection methods
   - Response procedures

5. **Risk Assessment**
   - Potential harms
   - Likelihood and severity
   - Affected populations
   - Ethical considerations

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Clear decision framing (25%)
- Appropriate method selection (25%)
- Comprehensive metric definition (25%)
- Thorough risk assessment (25%)

---

### Phase 2: Experiment/Causal Design (Week 2)

**Deliverable: Experiment/Causal Design Document**

**Components:**

1. **Method Selection**
   - A/B test, DiD, synthetic control, matching, or other
   - Justification for choice
   - Alternative methods considered

2. **Design Specification**

   **For A/B Tests:**
   - Randomization mechanism
   - Unit of randomization
   - Sample size calculation
   - Power analysis
   - MDE specification
   - Duration and traffic estimates

   **For Causal Inference:**
   - Natural variation identified
   - Control group construction
   - Key assumptions
   - Validation plan

3. **Implementation Plan**
   - Technical implementation
   - Feature flags or rollout mechanism
   - Data collection
   - Monitoring setup

4. **Analysis Plan**
   - Primary analysis method
   - Segment analysis plan
   - Guardrail monitoring
   - Decision criteria

5. **Safety Plan**
   - Guardrail monitoring
   - Kill switch procedures
   - Rollback strategy
   - Communication plan

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Sound methodological choice (30%)
- Proper design specification (30%)
- Complete implementation plan (20%)
- Comprehensive safety plan (20%)

---

### Phase 3: Analysis & Interpretation (Week 3)

**Deliverable: Analysis & Interpretation Document**

**Components:**

1. **Data Analysis**
   - Primary results
   - Statistical tests
   - Confidence intervals
   - Effect size estimates

2. **Segment Analysis**
   - Results by key segments
   - Heterogeneous effects
   - Simpson's paradox check

3. **Guardrail Assessment**
   - Guardrail metric results
   - Unintended consequences
   - Early warning signals

4. **Uncertainty Quantification**
   - Confidence intervals
   - Sensitivity analysis
   - Alternative explanations
   - Limitations

5. **Interpretation**
   - What do results mean?
   - Statistical vs practical significance
   - Business impact
   - Risk assessment

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Accurate statistical analysis (30%)
- Comprehensive segment analysis (25%)
- Proper uncertainty handling (25%)
- Clear interpretation (20%)

---

### Phase 4: Risk & Guardrail Assessment (Week 4)

**Deliverable: Risk & Guardrail Assessment Document**

**Components:**

1. **Guardrail Results**
   - All guardrail metrics assessed
   - Degradation detected?
   - Severity assessment

2. **Unintended Consequences**
   - Comprehensive check
   - Segment-level issues
   - Long-term concerns
   - User feedback review

3. **Risk Update**
   - Initial risk assessment updated
   - New risks identified
   - Risk severity reassessed

4. **Safety Recommendations**
   - Guardrail status
   - Safety concerns
   - Mitigation strategies
   - Rollback triggers

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Thorough guardrail assessment (30%)
- Comprehensive consequence detection (30%)
- Updated risk assessment (20%)
- Clear safety recommendations (20%)

---

### Phase 5: Final Rollout Recommendation (Week 5)

**Deliverable: Executive Rollout Memo**

**Components:**

1. **Executive Summary** (1 page)
   - Recommendation (first paragraph)
   - Key results
   - Business impact
   - Risks & mitigation
   - Next steps

2. **Complete Analysis Summary** (2-3 pages)
   - Experiment overview
   - Results summary
   - Interpretation
   - Uncertainty

3. **Decision Recommendation** (1-2 pages)
   - Clear recommendation (Rollout/Iterate/Kill)
   - Rationale with framework
   - Confidence level
   - Alternative options considered

4. **Implementation Plan** (1-2 pages)
   - Rollout strategy
   - Timeline
   - Resource requirements
   - Monitoring plan

5. **Risk & Mitigation** (1 page)
   - Final risk assessment
   - Mitigation strategies
   - Contingency plans
   - Rollback procedures

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Clear recommendation (25%)
- Effective communication (25%)
- Complete analysis (25%)
- Practical implementation (25%)

---

### Phase 6: Presentation & Defense (Week 6)

**Deliverable: Leadership-Style Review Presentation**

**Components:**

1. **Presentation** (15-20 minutes)
   - Decision context
   - Method and design
   - Results and interpretation
   - Recommendation
   - Q&A preparation

2. **Defense** (10-15 minutes)
   - Answer questions
   - Defend methodology
   - Justify recommendations
   - Discuss alternatives

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Clear presentation (30%)
- Strong defense (30%)
- Methodology justification (20%)
- Professional communication (20%)

## Project Options

### Option 1: Real Business Decision

**Use Your Own Organization:**
- Identify real decision needing causal analysis
- Apply course methods to actual problem
- Deliver actionable recommendation
- Maximum real-world impact

**Requirements:**
- Real data (or access to run experiment)
- Business stakeholder engagement
- Actual decision to be made
- Permission to share (anonymized if needed)

### Option 2: Case Study Analysis

**Use Provided Case Study:**
- Analyze historical experiment or decision
- Apply course methods retrospectively
- Provide recommendations
- Learn from real scenarios

**Requirements:**
- Case study data provided
- Historical context
- Known outcomes (for learning)
- Analysis and recommendations

### Option 3: Simulated Scenario

**Use Simulated Data:**
- Realistic business scenario
- Generated data with known effects
- Apply full methodology
- Practice end-to-end process

**Requirements:**
- Scenario description
- Simulated data
- Realistic constraints
- Full analysis and recommendations
