# Module 5: Portfolio Prioritization & Investment Decisions

**Duration:** Week 5  
**Theme:** Choosing what not to do  
**Learning Objectives:**
- Manage a portfolio of data, ML, and AI initiatives
- Apply prioritization frameworks (impact vs effort, risk-adjusted ROI)
- Balance innovation and reliability
- Plan capacity and sequencing
- Make decisions to kill projects and reallocate investment

---

## 5.1 Managing a Portfolio of Data, ML, and AI Initiatives

### The Portfolio Challenge

**The Problem:**
- More opportunities than resources
- Competing priorities
- Unclear trade-offs
- Difficulty saying "no"

**The Solution:**
- Manage initiatives as a portfolio
- Apply consistent prioritization
- Make explicit trade-offs
- Kill low-value initiatives

### Portfolio Categories

#### Category 1: Core Operations
**Definition:** Essential capabilities that must be maintained
**Examples:** Data infrastructure, model monitoring, analytics platforms
**Characteristics:** High reliability, low innovation, must-do
**Allocation:** 40-50% of resources

#### Category 2: Strategic Initiatives
**Definition:** High-impact initiatives aligned to strategy
**Examples:** Personalization, fraud detection, demand forecasting
**Characteristics:** High impact, strategic importance, must-win
**Allocation:** 30-40% of resources

#### Category 3: Innovation & Exploration
**Definition:** Emerging opportunities and experiments
**Examples:** GenAI pilots, new ML techniques, research
**Characteristics:** High uncertainty, potential high impact, optional
**Allocation:** 10-20% of resources

#### Category 4: Support & Maintenance
**Definition:** Ongoing support and incremental improvements
**Examples:** Bug fixes, performance optimization, minor enhancements
**Characteristics:** Low impact, necessary, keep-the-lights-on
**Allocation:** 10-15% of resources

### Portfolio Balance

**Healthy Portfolio:**
- ✅ Balanced across categories
- ✅ Aligned to strategy
- ✅ Appropriate risk profile
- ✅ Sustainable capacity

**Unhealthy Portfolio:**
- ❌ Too much in one category
- ❌ Misaligned with strategy
- ❌ Too risky or too conservative
- ❌ Over-committed

## 5.2 Prioritization Frameworks

### Framework 1: Impact vs Effort

**Structure:**
- **X-axis:** Effort (low to high)
- **Y-axis:** Impact (low to high)

**Quadrants:**
- **High Impact, Low Effort:** ✅ Quick Wins (Do First)
- **High Impact, High Effort:** 🔄 Major Projects (Plan Carefully)
- **Low Impact, Low Effort:** ⚠️ Fill-Ins (Do If Time)
- **Low Impact, High Effort:** ❌ Thankless Tasks (Avoid)

**Usage:**
- Plot all initiatives
- Prioritize by quadrant
- Focus on quick wins and major projects

### Framework 2: Risk-Adjusted ROI

**Formula:**
```
Risk-Adjusted ROI = (Expected Value × Probability of Success) / Investment
```

**Components:**
- **Expected Value:** Financial impact if successful
- **Probability of Success:** Likelihood of success (0-1)
- **Investment:** Cost (time, money, resources)

**Example:**
- Initiative A: $10M value, 80% success, $2M investment
- Risk-Adjusted ROI = ($10M × 0.8) / $2M = 4.0

- Initiative B: $20M value, 50% success, $5M investment
- Risk-Adjusted ROI = ($20M × 0.5) / $5M = 2.0

- **Decision:** Prioritize Initiative A (higher risk-adjusted ROI)

### Framework 3: Strategic Value Matrix

**Structure:**
- **X-axis:** Strategic Alignment (low to high)
- **Y-axis:** Business Impact (low to high)

**Quadrants:**
- **High Impact, High Alignment:** ✅ Strategic Priorities (Must Do)
- **High Impact, Low Alignment:** 🔄 Evaluate Alignment (Question)
- **Low Impact, High Alignment:** ⚠️ Nice to Have (Consider)
- **Low Impact, Low Alignment:** ❌ Don't Do (Avoid)

### Framework 4: ICE Score

**Formula:**
```
ICE Score = (Impact + Confidence + Ease) / 3
```

**Components:**
- **Impact:** 1-10 (business impact)
- **Confidence:** 1-10 (confidence in success)
- **Ease:** 1-10 (ease of implementation, reverse scale)

**Usage:**
- Score each initiative
- Rank by ICE score
- Prioritize top scores

### Choosing a Framework

**Impact vs Effort:**
- ✅ Simple and intuitive
- ✅ Good for initial prioritization
- ❌ Doesn't account for risk or strategy

**Risk-Adjusted ROI:**
- ✅ Accounts for uncertainty
- ✅ Financial focus
- ❌ Requires financial estimates

**Strategic Value Matrix:**
- ✅ Considers strategy alignment
- ✅ Good for strategic planning
- ❌ Subjective scoring

**ICE Score:**
- ✅ Simple and quick
- ✅ Good for many initiatives
- ❌ Subjective scoring

**Best Practice:** Use multiple frameworks and triangulate.

## 5.3 Balancing Innovation and Reliability

### The Tension

**Innovation:**
- New capabilities
- Emerging technologies
- Experimentation
- Risk-taking

**Reliability:**
- Stable systems
- Proven technologies
- Predictable outcomes
- Risk management

**Challenge:** Balance both without sacrificing either.

### The Balance Framework

#### Allocation by Horizon

**Horizon 1 (Now):** 80% Reliability, 20% Innovation
- Focus on reliable delivery
- Incremental innovation
- Proven technologies

**Horizon 2 (Next):** 60% Reliability, 40% Innovation
- Build new capabilities
- Explore emerging technologies
- Balanced approach

**Horizon 3 (Later):** 40% Reliability, 60% Innovation
- Research and exploration
- Emerging technologies
- High-risk, high-reward

#### Allocation by Category

**Core Operations:** 90% Reliability, 10% Innovation
- Stability is critical
- Incremental improvements only

**Strategic Initiatives:** 70% Reliability, 30% Innovation
- Balance reliability and innovation
- Use proven technologies with innovation

**Innovation & Exploration:** 20% Reliability, 80% Innovation
- High innovation, accept risk
- Learn and iterate

### Innovation Principles

**Principle 1: Time-Box Innovation**
- Set time limits for experiments
- Kill experiments that don't show promise
- Don't let innovation drag on

**Principle 2: Isolate Innovation**
- Keep innovation separate from production
- Don't risk reliability for innovation
- Use sandboxes and experiments

**Principle 3: Validate Before Scaling**
- Prove value before scaling
- Use experiments and pilots
- Don't over-invest in unproven ideas

**Principle 4: Learn from Failure**
- Accept that innovation fails
- Learn from failures
- Don't punish failure

### Reliability Principles

**Principle 1: Invest in Foundations**
- Build reliable foundations
- Don't cut corners on infrastructure
- Quality enables speed

**Principle 2: Monitor and Maintain**
- Continuous monitoring
- Proactive maintenance
- Don't let systems degrade

**Principle 3: Standardize and Automate**
- Standardize where possible
- Automate repetitive tasks
- Reduce variability

**Principle 4: Plan for Failure**
- Design for resilience
- Have backup plans
- Test failure scenarios

## 5.4 Capacity Planning and Sequencing

### Capacity Planning

**The Challenge:**
- Limited resources (people, time, budget)
- More work than capacity
- Unclear capacity limits

**The Solution:**
- Understand true capacity
- Plan for realistic capacity
- Buffer for uncertainty

### Capacity Calculation

**Formula:**
```
Capacity = (People × Time × Utilization) - Overhead
```

**Components:**
- **People:** Number of people
- **Time:** Available time (e.g., hours per week)
- **Utilization:** Actual utilization (typically 60-70%)
- **Overhead:** Meetings, admin, support (typically 20-30%)

**Example:**
- 10 people × 40 hours/week × 70% utilization = 280 hours/week
- Overhead: 20% = 56 hours/week
- **Capacity:** 224 hours/week

### Sequencing Principles

**Principle 1: Dependencies First**
- Do foundational work first
- Don't start dependent work until dependencies are ready
- Map dependencies clearly

**Principle 2: Quick Wins Early**
- Build momentum with quick wins
- Don't start with hardest work
- Show progress early

**Principle 3: Risk Reduction**
- Do risky work early to learn
- Don't leave risky work to the end
- Have time to recover

**Principle 4: Capacity Constraints**
- Don't over-commit
- Leave buffer for uncertainty
- Plan for capacity changes

### Sequencing Example

**Initiative Sequence:**
1. **Foundation (Months 1-2):** Data infrastructure
2. **Quick Win (Months 2-3):** Analytics dashboard
3. **Strategic (Months 3-6):** Personalization model
4. **Innovation (Months 6-9):** GenAI pilot
5. **Scale (Months 9-12):** Scale successful initiatives

**Rationale:**
- Foundation enables everything
- Quick win builds momentum
- Strategic delivers value
- Innovation explores future
- Scale amplifies success

## 5.5 Killing Projects and Reallocating Investment

### The Challenge

**Why It's Hard:**
- Sunk cost fallacy
- Emotional attachment
- Fear of admitting failure
- Political pressure

**Why It's Necessary:**
- Limited resources
- Opportunity cost
- Focus on what matters
- Learning from failure

### When to Kill a Project

**Signals:**
1. **Low Value:** Not delivering expected value
2. **Low Probability:** Low likelihood of success
3. **Misalignment:** No longer aligned to strategy
4. **Better Alternatives:** Better opportunities available
5. **Resource Constraints:** Need resources elsewhere

**Decision Framework:**
- **Kill if:** Low value AND low probability
- **Pause if:** Low value OR low probability (reassess)
- **Continue if:** High value AND high probability

### The Kill Process

**Step 1: Assess**
- Review project status
- Evaluate value and probability
- Compare to alternatives

**Step 2: Decide**
- Make kill decision
- Document rationale
- Get alignment

**Step 3: Communicate**
- Communicate decision clearly
- Explain rationale
- Acknowledge contributions

**Step 4: Reallocate**
- Free up resources
- Reallocate to higher-value work
- Learn from experience

### Reallocation Principles

**Principle 1: Act Quickly**
- Don't delay kill decisions
- Every day is opportunity cost
- Free up resources fast

**Principle 2: Learn from Failure**
- Understand why it failed
- Document learnings
- Apply to future projects

**Principle 3: Reallocate Strategically**
- Don't just reallocate randomly
- Reallocate to highest-value work
- Align to strategy

**Principle 4: Communicate Clearly**
- Explain reallocation rationale
- Show how resources will be used
- Get alignment

### Example: Project Kill and Reallocation

**Project to Kill:**
- **Project:** Legacy system migration
- **Status:** 6 months behind, over budget
- **Value:** Low (legacy system works)
- **Probability:** Low (technical challenges)
- **Decision:** Kill

**Reallocation:**
- **From:** Legacy migration (3 people, 6 months)
- **To:** Personalization initiative (higher value, higher probability)
- **Impact:** +$5M revenue potential

## Summary

**Key Takeaways:**

1. **Portfolio Management:** Manage initiatives as a portfolio across categories (core, strategic, innovation, support). Balance allocation.

2. **Prioritization Frameworks:** Use multiple frameworks (impact vs effort, risk-adjusted ROI, strategic value, ICE) and triangulate. Focus on high-impact, high-probability initiatives.

3. **Innovation vs Reliability:** Balance innovation and reliability by horizon and category. Time-box innovation, isolate from production, validate before scaling.

4. **Capacity Planning:** Understand true capacity, plan realistically, buffer for uncertainty. Sequence by dependencies, quick wins, risk reduction.

5. **Kill Decisions:** Kill low-value, low-probability projects. Act quickly, learn from failure, reallocate strategically.
