## Lesson 3.1: Primary vs Secondary Authority Sources

### The Authority Hierarchy

AI models prioritize sources differently by vertical. Understanding this hierarchy is critical for visibility.

**Primary Authority Sources:**
- Highest trust and citation frequency
- Industry-specific credibility
- Regulatory or professional validation
- Essential for visibility in regulated verticals

**Secondary Authority Sources:**
- Supporting citations
- Helpful but not mandatory
- Can enhance visibility
- Important for competitive positioning

### Primary Authority by Vertical

**Healthcare:**
- Medical journals (peer-reviewed)
- Regulatory bodies (FDA, EMA)
- Clinical guidelines
- Medical associations
- Licensed professionals

**Finance:**
- Regulatory filings (SEC, FINRA)
- Financial reports (10-K, 10-Q)
- Regulatory bodies
- Licensed professionals
- Industry standards

**Legal:**
- Case law
- Statutes and regulations
- Legal precedents
- Bar associations
- Licensed attorneys

**E-commerce:**
- Product data (structured)
- User reviews (aggregated)
- Comparison sites
- Brand websites
- Product specifications

**Technology:**
- Technical documentation
- Open source repositories
- Industry standards
- Technical blogs (expert)
- Developer communities

### Secondary Authority by Vertical

**Healthcare:**
- Patient testimonials
- Health blogs
- Wellness websites
- Consumer reviews

**Finance:**
- Financial news
- Analyst reports
- Investment blogs
- Consumer reviews

**Legal:**
- Legal blogs
- Law firm websites
- Legal news
- Consumer guides

**E-commerce:**
- Social media
- Influencer content
- Marketing materials
- Brand content

**Technology:**
- Tech news
- Product reviews
- Community forums
- Tutorial sites

## Lesson 3.2: The Role of Standards Bodies, Regulators, and Trade Publications

### Standards Bodies

**Definition:** Organizations that establish industry standards and best practices

**Examples:**
- ISO (International Organization for Standardization)
- IEEE (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers)
- W3C (World Wide Web Consortium)
- Industry-specific standards organizations

**AI Treatment:**
- High authority
- Frequently cited
- Trusted for technical standards
- Referenced for compliance

**Strategy:**
- Align with standards
- Get certified
- Reference standards in content
- Build relationships with standards bodies

### Regulators

**Definition:** Government agencies that regulate industries

**Examples:**
- FDA (Food and Drug Administration)
- SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission)
- FTC (Federal Trade Commission)
- Industry-specific regulators

**AI Treatment:**
- Highest authority in regulated verticals
- Frequently cited for compliance
- Trusted for safety and regulation
- Referenced for legal requirements

**Strategy:**
- Ensure compliance
- Reference regulatory guidance
- Align content with regulations
- Monitor regulatory updates

### Trade Publications

**Definition:** Industry-specific publications and media

**Examples:**
- Medical journals
- Financial publications
- Legal periodicals
- Industry magazines
- Trade websites

**AI Treatment:**
- High authority in vertical
- Frequently cited
- Trusted for industry insights
- Referenced for trends

**Strategy:**
- Get featured in trade publications
- Build relationships with editors
- Provide expert commentary
- Contribute thought leadership

## Lesson 3.4: How Competitors Hijack Authority

### Authority Displacement

**The Problem:**
Competitors can position themselves as authorities even when you have superior credentials.

**Methods:**

1. **Citation Farming:**
   - Getting cited in high-authority sources
   - Building citation density
   - Displacing competitors from citations

2. **Authority Mimicry:**
   - Aligning content with authority sources
   - Using similar language patterns
   - Referencing same standards/regulations

3. **Source Co-optation:**
   - Getting featured in trade publications
   - Building relationships with standards bodies
   - Becoming source for media

4. **Content Displacement:**
   - Creating content that AI prefers
   - Optimizing for citation patterns
   - Displacing competitor content

### Examples

**Healthcare Example:**

**Your Brand:**
- Licensed medical professionals
- Peer-reviewed research
- Regulatory compliance

**Competitor:**
- Gets cited in medical journals
- Featured in trade publications
- Referenced by standards bodies
- **Result:** Competitor displaces you in citations

**E-commerce Example:**

**Your Brand:**
- Superior product quality
- Better user reviews
- Strong product data

**Competitor:**
- Aggressive citation farming
- Higher citation density
- Better structured data
- **Result:** Competitor gets more citations

### Defense Strategies

1. **Build Citation Density:**
   - Increase citations in high-authority sources
   - Improve citation frequency
   - Compete on citation volume

2. **Strengthen Authority Signals:**
   - Enhance credentials
   - Build industry recognition
   - Align with standards/regulations

3. **Monitor Competitive Citations:**
   - Track competitor citations
   - Identify displacement patterns
   - Respond to competitive threats

4. **Optimize Content for Citations:**
   - Align with citation patterns
   - Improve content quality
   - Enhance authority signals

## Practical Exercise 3: AI Citation Landscape by Vertical

### Objective
Map the citation landscape for your vertical to understand which sources AI trusts and how to build authority.

### Steps

#### Step 1: Citation Source Analysis (90 minutes)

1. **Test 50 Industry Queries:**
   - Document all citations in AI responses
   - Categorize citation sources
   - Identify citation patterns

2. **Categorize Sources:**
   - Primary authority sources
   - Secondary authority sources
   - PR/media sources
   - Product data sources
   - UGC sources

3. **Analyze Citation Frequency:**
   - Which sources are cited most
   - Which sources are cited least
   - Citation patterns by query type

#### Step 2: Authority Hierarchy Mapping (60 minutes)

1. **Create Authority Hierarchy:**
   - Rank sources by citation frequency
   - Identify primary vs secondary
   - Map authority levels

2. **Document Source Types:**
   - Standards bodies
   - Regulators
   - Trade publications
   - Media
   - UGC platforms

3. **Identify Gaps:**
   - Sources you're not in
   - Sources competitors dominate
   - Opportunities for authority building

#### Step 3: PR vs Product Data vs UGC Analysis (45 minutes)

1. **Measure Impact:**
   - PR citation frequency
   - Product data citation frequency
   - UGC citation frequency

2. **Compare to Competitors:**
   - Your PR citations vs competitors
   - Your product data citations vs competitors
   - Your UGC citations vs competitors

3. **Identify Opportunities:**
   - Which type to prioritize
   - Where to invest resources
   - How to improve citations

#### Step 4: Competitive Citation Analysis (60 minutes)

1. **Track Competitor Citations:**
   - Which competitors are cited
   - How frequently
   - In what contexts

2. **Identify Displacement Patterns:**
   - Where competitors displace you
   - How they build authority
   - What strategies they use

3. **Document Threats:**
   - Competitive citation threats
   - Authority displacement risks
   - Visibility risks

#### Step 5: Source Influence Weighting Model (45 minutes)

1. **Create Weighting Model:**
   - Assign weights to source types
   - Calculate influence scores
   - Rank sources by influence

2. **Calculate Your Authority Score:**
   - Your citations × source weights
   - Your authority score
   - Competitive comparison

3. **Set Authority Goals:**
   - Target authority score
   - Source coverage goals
   - Citation frequency goals

### Deliverables

1. **Citation Source Inventory:**
   - Complete list of cited sources
   - Source categorization
   - Citation frequency analysis

2. **Authority Hierarchy Map:**
   - Visual hierarchy
   - Primary vs secondary sources
   - Authority levels

3. **PR/Product Data/UGC Analysis:**
   - Impact measurement
   - Competitive comparison
   - Opportunity identification

4. **Competitive Citation Report:**
   - Competitor citation analysis
   - Displacement patterns
   - Threat assessment

5. **Source Influence Model:**
   - Weighting model
   - Authority score calculation
   - Goal setting

### Evaluation Criteria

- **Completeness:** All sources documented
- **Accuracy:** Citation patterns correctly identified
- **Depth:** Analysis goes beyond surface level
- **Actionability:** Strategies are specific and achievable
- **Insight:** Clear understanding of citation landscape
