# Monitoring the New Scoreboard

## Overview

Traditional SEO metrics like traffic and clicks are becoming less relevant in the AI era. This module introduces new KPIs for measuring AI visibility, tracking visibility decay, and understanding attribution in a fragmented customer journey.

## Learning Objectives

- Identify and track new KPIs for AI visibility
- Monitor visibility decay and model volatility
- Measure attribution in fragmented customer journeys

---

## Lesson 6.1: New KPIs for the AI Era

### The Limitations of Traditional Metrics

Traditional SEO metrics focus on:
- **Traffic**: Page views and sessions
- **Clicks**: Click-through rates from search
- **Rankings**: Position in search results
- **Conversions**: Actions taken on your site

**Problem**: These metrics don't capture AI visibility, where:
- Users get answers without clicking
- Citations happen without traffic
- Visibility exists without rankings
- Discovery happens off your site

### New AI Visibility KPIs

#### 1. AI Visibility Score

**Definition**: How frequently your brand/content appears in AI-generated responses across all models.

**Measurement**:
- Test a set of relevant queries across major AI models
- Count how many times you're cited or mentioned
- Calculate as a percentage of total queries tested
- Track over time to measure trends

**Example**:
```
AI Visibility Score = (Number of citations / Total queries tested) × 100

If you're cited in 45 out of 100 test queries:
AI Visibility Score = 45%
```

#### 2. Citation Frequency

**Definition**: How often your brand, products, or expertise are cited as sources in AI responses.

**Measurement**:
- Track citations across different AI models
- Categorize by topic/query type
- Measure citation prominence (mentioned first vs. last)
- Compare to competitors

**Tracking**:
```
Citation Frequency = Number of citations per time period

Weekly Citation Report:
- ChatGPT: 12 citations
- Claude: 8 citations
- Perplexity: 15 citations
- Google AI Overviews: 10 citations
Total: 45 citations/week
```

#### 3. Mention Share

**Definition**: Your share of mentions compared to competitors in AI responses for your industry/keywords.

**Calculation**:
```
Mention Share = (Your mentions / Total industry mentions) × 100

Example:
- Your brand: 25 mentions
- Competitor A: 30 mentions
- Competitor B: 20 mentions
- Competitor C: 15 mentions
Total: 90 mentions

Your Mention Share = (25 / 90) × 100 = 27.8%
```

#### 4. Citation Quality Score

**Definition**: Assessment of how positively and accurately you're cited.

**Factors**:
- **Accuracy**: Is the information correct?
- **Completeness**: Is your full value proposition represented?
- **Context**: Are you cited in relevant, valuable contexts?
- **Attribution**: Is your expertise/brand properly attributed?

**Scoring**:
- +2: Highly positive, accurate, complete citation
- +1: Positive, mostly accurate citation
- 0: Neutral or mixed citation
- -1: Negative or inaccurate citation
- -2: Highly negative or false citation

#### 5. Entity Recognition Rate

**Definition**: How consistently AI models recognize and correctly identify your brand as an entity.

**Measurement**:
- Test entity queries ("What is [Brand]?")
- Measure correct identification vs. confusion with competitors
- Track entity relationship recognition (your brand → your products)
- Monitor entity description accuracy

#### 6. Topic Authority Score

**Definition**: Your visibility and authority across different topic areas.

**Measurement**:
- Test queries across different topics relevant to your business
- Measure citation frequency per topic
- Identify topic gaps (where you should be cited but aren't)
- Track topic authority trends over time

**Example**:
```
Topic Authority Report:
- Topic A (Core expertise): 85% visibility
- Topic B (Related area): 60% visibility
- Topic C (Emerging area): 30% visibility
- Topic D (Should own): 15% visibility ← Gap identified
```

#### 7. Cross-Model Consistency

**Definition**: How consistently you're cited across different AI models.

**Measurement**:
- Test same queries across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Google
- Measure consistency of information
- Identify model-specific gaps
- Track consistency over time

### Implementing AI Visibility Tracking

#### Step 1: Define Your Query Set

Create a comprehensive list of queries to test:

**Brand Queries**:
- "What is [Your Brand]?"
- "Who is [Your Brand]?"
- "[Your Brand] reviews"

**Product Queries**:
- "Best [product category]"
- "[Your Product] features"
- "[Your Product] vs [Competitor]"

**Topic Queries**:
- "[Your expertise area]"
- "How to [relevant action]"
- "[Industry] best practices"

**Competitive Queries**:
- "[Your Brand] vs [Competitor]"
- "Alternatives to [Your Brand]"

#### Step 2: Establish Baseline

Test all queries across major AI models and document:
- Current citation frequency
- Mention share vs. competitors
- Citation quality
- Topic coverage

#### Step 3: Regular Monitoring

- **Weekly**: Test key brand/product queries
- **Monthly**: Comprehensive query set testing
- **Quarterly**: Full competitive analysis
- **As needed**: After content launches or optimizations

#### Step 4: Track Trends

Create dashboards showing:
- AI Visibility Score over time
- Citation frequency trends
- Mention share changes
- Topic authority evolution
- Cross-model consistency

### Key Takeaways

- Traditional metrics don't capture AI visibility
- New KPIs focus on citations, mentions, and entity recognition
- Regular monitoring across multiple AI models is essential
- Track trends to measure optimization effectiveness

## Lesson 6.2: Tracking Visibility Decay

### What is Visibility Decay?

Visibility decay occurs when your brand gradually drops out of AI models' consideration sets. This can happen due to:

- **Content staleness**: Information becomes outdated
- **Competitive pressure**: Competitors gain more visibility
- **Model updates**: AI training data changes
- **Algorithm shifts**: How models prioritize sources changes
- **Negative signals**: Poor reviews or misinformation

### Why Visibility Decay Matters

AI models have **volatile responses**—what they cite today may not be what they cite next month. This volatility means:

- Visibility can disappear quickly
- Regular monitoring is essential
- Proactive maintenance is required
- Competitive advantage can erode rapidly

### Monthly Volatility Monitoring

#### The Volatility Index

Track how much AI responses change month-over-month:

```
Volatility Index = (Changed responses / Total responses) × 100

Example:
- January: 100 test queries, 45 citations
- February: 100 test queries, 38 citations (7 lost)
- March: 100 test queries, 42 citations (4 regained, 2 new)

Monthly Volatility = 13% (13 out of 100 queries changed)
```

#### Tracking Citation Changes

For each query, track:
- **Stable**: Cited consistently month-over-month
- **Gained**: New citation this month
- **Lost**: Citation disappeared this month
- **Changed**: Still cited, but context/accuracy changed

### Detecting Visibility Decay

#### Early Warning Signs

1. **Declining Citation Frequency**
   - Fewer citations month-over-month
   - Loss of citations in key queries
   - Reduced mention share

2. **Topic Authority Erosion**
   - Losing citations in core expertise areas
   - Competitors gaining in your topics
   - New topics not being captured

3. **Cross-Model Inconsistency**
   - Cited in some models but not others
   - Inconsistent information across models
   - Model-specific visibility drops

4. **Citation Quality Decline**
   - Less accurate citations
   - Reduced context in mentions
   - Negative or neutral shifts in sentiment

#### Decay Patterns to Watch

**Gradual Decay**:
- Slow, steady decline over months
- Often due to content staleness
- Requires content refresh strategy

**Sudden Drop**:
- Rapid visibility loss
- May indicate algorithm change or negative signal
- Requires immediate investigation

**Selective Decay**:
- Loss in specific topics or queries
- May indicate competitive pressure
- Requires topic-specific optimization

**Model-Specific Decay**:
- Decline in one AI model but not others
- May indicate model update or training data change
- Requires model-specific strategy

### Preventing Visibility Decay

#### 1. Content Freshness

- **Regular Updates**: Refresh content quarterly
- **Current Information**: Keep facts, statistics, and data current
- **Recent Examples**: Include recent case studies and examples
- **Date Stamps**: Show when content was last updated

#### 2. Ongoing Citation Building

- **Continuous Optimization**: Don't stop after initial gains
- **New Content**: Regularly publish new, citable content
- **Expert Quotes**: Continuously add authoritative quotations
- **Statistics**: Update with latest data and research

#### 3. Competitive Monitoring

- **Track Competitors**: Monitor their visibility gains
- **Identify Gaps**: Find where they're winning
- **Respond Strategically**: Optimize in areas of competitive pressure

#### 4. Model-Specific Optimization

- **Test Regularly**: Monitor all major models
- **Identify Gaps**: Find model-specific visibility issues
- **Optimize Accordingly**: Address model-specific needs

### Visibility Decay Response Protocol

#### When Decay is Detected

1. **Immediate** (Day 1):
   - Document the decay (queries, models, extent)
   - Compare to previous month's data
   - Identify potential causes

2. **Short-term** (Week 1):
   - Update stale content
   - Add fresh statistics and examples
   - Publish new citable content
   - Fix any accuracy issues

3. **Medium-term** (Month 1):
   - Comprehensive content refresh
   - Competitive analysis and response
   - Model-specific optimization
   - Enhanced citation building

4. **Long-term** (Quarter 1):
   - Strategic content planning
   - Ongoing monitoring system
   - Preventive maintenance schedule
   - Continuous optimization process

### Measuring Recovery

Track recovery metrics:
- **Time to Recovery**: How long to regain lost visibility
- **Recovery Rate**: Percentage of lost citations regained
- **New Gains**: Citations in new queries/topics
- **Stability**: Maintaining visibility after recovery

### Key Takeaways

- Visibility decay is real and can happen quickly
- Monthly volatility monitoring is essential
- Early detection enables rapid response
- Preventive maintenance prevents decay

## Lesson 6.3: Attribution in a Fragmented Journey

### The Fragmented Customer Journey

Traditional customer journey:
```
Search → Click → Visit → Convert
```

AI-era customer journey:
```
AI Discovery → Research → Brand Search → Visit → Convert
         ↓
    (No direct click)
```

### The Attribution Challenge

When users discover your brand through AI but don't click:

- **No direct referral**: AI doesn't send traffic
- **Delayed action**: User may search later
- **Multiple touchpoints**: Journey spans platforms
- **Hidden influence**: AI impact is invisible

### Measuring AI Influence

#### 1. Brand Search Lift

**Concept**: AI mentions drive brand searches, even without direct clicks.

**Measurement**:
- Track branded search volume
- Correlate with AI visibility scores
- Measure lift after AI citations
- Compare to baseline periods

**Example**:
```
Baseline: 1,000 brand searches/month
After AI visibility increase: 1,400 brand searches/month
Brand Search Lift: +40%
```

#### 2. Assisted Conversions

**Concept**: AI discovery assists conversions even if not the final touchpoint.

**Measurement**:
- Track user paths that include AI discovery
- Measure conversions with AI in the path
- Attribute partial credit to AI influence
- Compare to direct conversions

#### 3. Time-to-Conversion Analysis

**Concept**: AI discovery may shorten or lengthen time to conversion.

**Measurement**:
- Track time from AI mention to conversion
- Compare to other discovery methods
- Identify patterns in conversion timing
- Measure impact on sales cycle

#### 4. Cross-Platform Attribution

**Concept**: Users discover via AI, research elsewhere, convert on your site.

**Measurement**:
- Track user behavior across platforms
- Identify AI → Research → Convert paths
- Measure platform influence
- Attribute credit across touchpoints

### Attribution Models for AI

#### Model 1: Last-Click Attribution (Traditional)

**Problem**: Doesn't credit AI discovery if user clicks from search later.

**Limitation**: Underestimates AI influence.

#### Model 2: First-Touch Attribution

**Gives credit to**: First touchpoint in the journey.

**Use case**: Measuring AI's role in initial discovery.

#### Model 3: Multi-Touch Attribution

**Gives credit to**: All touchpoints in the journey.

**Distribution examples**:
- Equal weight: 25% each for 4 touchpoints
- Time decay: More weight to recent touchpoints
- Position-based: More weight to first and last

#### Model 4: AI-Influenced Attribution

**Gives credit to**: AI discovery + subsequent touchpoints.

**Example**:
- AI discovery: 30%
- Brand search: 25%
- Website visit: 25%
- Conversion: 20%

### Tracking Fragmented Journeys

#### Method 1: Survey-Based Attribution

Ask users how they discovered your brand:
- "How did you first hear about us?"
- Include "AI assistant (ChatGPT, etc.)" as option
- Track responses over time
- Correlate with conversion data

#### Method 2: Correlation Analysis

Correlate AI visibility with business metrics:
- Brand search volume
- Direct traffic increases
- Conversion rate changes
- Revenue trends

**Example**:
```
AI Visibility Score ↑ 20% → Brand Searches ↑ 15% → Revenue ↑ 10%
Correlation suggests AI influence
```

#### Method 3: Experimental Design

Test AI visibility impact:
- **Control**: Baseline AI visibility
- **Treatment**: Increased AI visibility (through optimization)
- **Measure**: Business metric changes
- **Analyze**: Causal impact of AI visibility

#### Method 4: Advanced Analytics

Use analytics tools to track:
- User paths and touchpoints
- Time between touchpoints
- Conversion attribution
- Cross-device behavior

### Building an Attribution Framework

#### Step 1: Define Touchpoints

List all potential touchpoints:
- AI discovery (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity)
- Brand search (Google, Bing)
- Direct visit
- Social media
- Email
- Referral sites

#### Step 2: Assign Attribution Weights

Based on your business model:
- Discovery touchpoints: Higher weight
- Research touchpoints: Medium weight
- Conversion touchpoints: Lower weight (but still credit)

#### Step 3: Track Multi-Touch Paths

Use analytics to identify:
- Common paths to conversion
- AI's role in those paths
- Time between touchpoints
- Conversion rates by path

#### Step 4: Measure and Optimize

- Track attribution metrics monthly
- Adjust weights based on data
- Optimize for high-value paths
- Test attribution model changes

### Key Metrics for Fragmented Attribution

1. **AI-Influenced Conversions**: Conversions where AI was in the path
2. **Brand Search Conversion Rate**: Conversions from brand searches (may be AI-influenced)
3. **Time-to-Conversion**: Average time from AI discovery to conversion
4. **Multi-Touch Conversion Rate**: Conversions with multiple touchpoints
5. **Attribution Value**: Revenue attributed to AI influence

### Key Takeaways

- Customer journeys are fragmented across platforms
- AI discovery influences conversions without direct clicks
- Multi-touch attribution models better capture AI influence
- Correlation and experimental methods help measure impact

## Module Summary

Monitoring AI visibility requires:

1. **New KPIs**: AI Visibility Score, Citation Frequency, Mention Share, and more
2. **Decay Tracking**: Monthly volatility monitoring to detect and prevent visibility loss
3. **Fragmented Attribution**: Multi-touch models to measure AI's influence on conversions

The new scoreboard focuses on visibility, citations, and influence rather than just traffic and clicks. Regular monitoring and proactive optimization are essential to maintain and grow AI visibility in this volatile landscape.
