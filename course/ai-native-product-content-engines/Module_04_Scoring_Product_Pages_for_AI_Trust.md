# Module 4: Scoring Product Pages for AI Trust

**Truthfulness, Completeness, Evidence Density**

**Duration:** Week 4  
**Learning Objectives:**
- Learn how to evaluate PDPs the way AI does
- Replace subjective content reviews with scoring systems
- Understand truthfulness: claims vs verifiable support
- Learn completeness: who should buy vs shouldn't
- Master evidence density: specs, comparisons, visuals, constraints
- Recognize risk of omission vs over-claiming

---

## 4.1 The AI Evaluation Framework

### Why Subjective Reviews Fail

**Traditional Content Review:**
- "Does this sound good?"
- "Is the tone right?"
- "Does this match brand voice?"
- **Problem:** Subjective, not measurable, doesn't predict AI performance

### The AI Scoring System

**AI evaluates PDPs on three dimensions:**

**1. Truthfulness**
- Are claims supported by evidence?
- Can claims be verified?
- Do claims match specifications?
- Are there contradictions?

**2. Completeness**
- Is it clear who should buy?
- Is it clear who shouldn't buy?
- Are use cases defined?
- Are constraints acknowledged?

**3. Evidence Density**
- How much evidence supports claims?
- Are there multiple evidence types?
- Is evidence specific and measurable?
- Can evidence be cross-referenced?

## 4.2 Truthfulness: Claims vs Verifiable Support

### The Claim-Evidence Gap

**Low Truthfulness:**
- Claims without evidence
- Vague claims that can't be verified
- Claims that contradict specifications
- Unsupported superlatives

**High Truthfulness:**
- Claims with specific evidence
- Measurable, verifiable statements
- Claims that match specifications
- Evidence-backed differentiation

### Truthfulness Scoring

**For each claim, score:**

**1. Evidence Support (0-10)**
- 10: Specific, measurable evidence (e.g., "40mm drivers, frequency response 20Hz-20kHz")
- 5: General evidence (e.g., "High-quality drivers")
- 0: No evidence (e.g., "Premium sound quality")

**2. Verifiability (0-10)**
- 10: Can be independently verified (specs, test results)
- 5: Can be partially verified (customer reviews)
- 0: Cannot be verified (subjective claims)

**3. Specification Alignment (0-10)**
- 10: Claims match specifications exactly
- 5: Claims partially match specifications
- 0: Claims contradict specifications

**4. Contradiction Risk (0-10)**
- 10: No contradictions with reviews/specs
- 5: Minor contradictions
- 0: Major contradictions

### Truthfulness Examples

**Low Truthfulness:**
> "Our headphones deliver unparalleled audio quality with premium materials and innovative design."

**Problems:**
- "Unparalleled" - no comparison evidence
- "Premium materials" - not specified
- "Innovative design" - not defined
- No verifiable support

**High Truthfulness:**
> "Our headphones use 40mm dynamic drivers with frequency response 20Hz-20kHz. The ear cups are made from memory foam and protein leather. The design includes a foldable hinge for portability."

**Strengths:**
- Specific, measurable claims
- Verifiable specifications
- No unsupported superlatives
- Evidence-rich statements

## 4.3 Completeness: Who Should Buy vs Shouldn't

### The Completeness Problem

**Incomplete PDPs:**
- Don't define target users
- Don't acknowledge constraints
- Don't specify use cases
- Don't mention tradeoffs

**Complete PDPs:**
- Clearly define who should buy
- Explicitly state who shouldn't buy
- Specify ideal use cases
- Acknowledge limitations

### Completeness Scoring

**1. Target User Clarity (0-10)**
- 10: Specific user profile defined (e.g., "Ideal for professionals who need noise cancellation for travel")
- 5: General user profile (e.g., "Good for music lovers")
- 0: No user profile (e.g., "Perfect for everyone")

**2. Constraint Acknowledgment (0-10)**
- 10: Explicit constraints stated (e.g., "Not recommended for users with small ears")
- 5: Implied constraints (e.g., "May be tight for some users")
- 0: No constraints mentioned

**3. Use Case Definition (0-10)**
- 10: Specific use cases with evidence (e.g., "Ideal for video editing with 4K displays")
- 5: General use cases (e.g., "Good for work")
- 0: No use cases mentioned

**4. Tradeoff Acknowledgment (0-10)**
- 10: Explicit tradeoffs stated (e.g., "Better battery life but heavier than competitors")
- 5: Implied tradeoffs
- 0: No tradeoffs mentioned

### Completeness Examples

**Incomplete:**
> "This laptop is perfect for everyone. It has great performance and is lightweight."

**Problems:**
- "Perfect for everyone" - no targeting
- No constraints mentioned
- No use cases defined
- No tradeoffs acknowledged

**Complete:**
> "Ideal for professionals who need portability and performance for video editing, but not recommended for gaming due to integrated graphics. Tradeoff: Excellent battery life (12 hours) but heavier (4.2 lbs) than ultraportables. Best for: Content creators, business travelers. Not ideal for: Gamers, users needing maximum portability."

**Strengths:**
- Specific target users
- Explicit constraints
- Defined use cases
- Acknowledged tradeoffs

## 4.4 Evidence Density: Specs, Comparisons, Visuals, Constraints

### What Is Evidence Density?

**Evidence Density = Amount of verifiable, specific information per claim**

**High Evidence Density:**
- Multiple evidence types per claim
- Specific, measurable data
- Cross-referenced information
- Visual and textual evidence

**Low Evidence Density:**
- Claims without evidence
- Vague or emotional language
- Single evidence type
- No cross-referencing

### Evidence Types

**1. Specifications**
- Objective, measurable attributes
- Standardized formats
- Technical data
- **Example:** "40mm drivers, 20Hz-20kHz frequency response, 350g weight"

**2. Comparisons**
- Side-by-side data
- Objective metrics
- Alternative options
- **Example:** "Battery life: 30 hours vs. competitor's 20 hours"

**3. Visual Evidence**
- Product images with scale
- Usage demonstrations
- Comparison visuals
- **Example:** Image showing product next to common object for scale

**4. Constraint Evidence**
- Limitations documented
- Tradeoffs explained
- Failure modes acknowledged
- **Example:** "Not recommended for users with small ears (ear cup diameter: 4.5cm)"

**5. Verification Evidence**
- Customer reviews with details
- Third-party test results
- Certifications
- **Example:** "4.5/5 stars from 1,000+ verified purchases, with 85% mentioning comfort"

### Evidence Density Scoring

**For each major claim, count:**

**1. Evidence Types (0-5)**
- 5: 4+ evidence types
- 3: 2-3 evidence types
- 1: 1 evidence type
- 0: No evidence

**2. Specificity (0-10)**
- 10: Highly specific, measurable (e.g., "40mm drivers")
- 5: Moderately specific (e.g., "Large drivers")
- 0: Vague (e.g., "Good sound")

**3. Cross-Reference (0-10)**
- 10: Multiple sources confirm claim
- 5: Single source confirms claim
- 0: No cross-reference

**4. Visual Support (0-10)**
- 10: Visual evidence supports claim
- 5: Visual evidence partially supports
- 0: No visual evidence

## 4.5 Risk of Omission vs Over-Claiming

### The Two Trust Risks

**1. Risk of Omission**
- Missing critical information
- Not acknowledging constraints
- Failing to define use cases
- Omitting comparisons

**2. Risk of Over-Claiming**
- Exaggerated claims
- Unsupported superlatives
- Contradictions with evidence
- False differentiation

### Omission Risk Scoring

**Missing Information Types:**
- Target user definition
- Constraint acknowledgment
- Use case specification
- Comparison context
- Tradeoff explanation

**Omission Risk Score:**
- Count missing information types
- Weight by importance
- Calculate risk level

### Over-Claiming Risk Scoring

**Over-Claiming Indicators:**
- Unsupported superlatives
- Vague differentiation
- Contradictions with reviews
- Exaggerated performance claims

**Over-Claiming Risk Score:**
- Count over-claiming indicators
- Weight by severity
- Calculate risk level

### Balancing Risks

**Optimal Strategy:**
- High truthfulness (evidence-backed claims)
- High completeness (constraints acknowledged)
- High evidence density (multiple evidence types)
- Low omission risk (critical info included)
- Low over-claiming risk (honest, verifiable claims)

## 4.6 The AI PDP Scorecard

### Scoring Framework

**Overall Score = Weighted Average of:**

**1. Truthfulness (30%)**
- Evidence support: 25%
- Verifiability: 25%
- Specification alignment: 25%
- Contradiction risk: 25%

**2. Completeness (30%)**
- Target user clarity: 25%
- Constraint acknowledgment: 25%
- Use case definition: 25%
- Tradeoff acknowledgment: 25%

**3. Evidence Density (40%)**
- Evidence types: 25%
- Specificity: 25%
- Cross-reference: 25%
- Visual support: 25%

### Score Interpretation

**90-100: Excellent**
- AI will trust and recommend
- High evidence density
- Complete and truthful

**70-89: Good**
- AI will generally trust
- Some evidence gaps
- Minor improvements needed

**50-69: Fair**
- AI may have trust issues
- Significant evidence gaps
- Major improvements needed

**Below 50: Poor**
- AI will likely not recommend
- Low evidence density
- Incomplete or untruthful

### Risk Assessment

**Low Risk:**
- Score 70+
- Low omission risk
- Low over-claiming risk

**Medium Risk:**
- Score 50-69
- Moderate omission risk
- Moderate over-claiming risk

**High Risk:**
- Score below 50
- High omission risk
- High over-claiming risk

## Lab 4: AI PDP Scorecard

### Objective
Evaluate your PDPs the way AI does using the truthfulness, completeness, and evidence density framework.

### Tasks

**Task 1: Truthfulness Scoring**
For 3 flagship PDPs:
1. Extract all major claims
2. Score each claim for evidence support, verifiability, specification alignment, contradiction risk
3. Calculate truthfulness score
4. Identify top 5 truthfulness gaps

**Task 2: Completeness Scoring**
For each PDP:
1. Score target user clarity, constraint acknowledgment, use case definition, tradeoff acknowledgment
2. Calculate completeness score
3. Identify missing information types
4. Prioritize completeness improvements

**Task 3: Evidence Density Scoring**
For each PDP:
1. Count evidence types per major claim
2. Score specificity, cross-reference, visual support
3. Calculate evidence density score
4. Identify evidence gaps

**Task 4: Risk Assessment**
For each PDP:
1. Calculate omission risk
2. Calculate over-claiming risk
3. Determine overall risk level
4. Create risk mitigation plan

**Task 5: Baseline Scores**
Create baseline scores for:
- Top 10 priority SKUs
- By product category
- Overall portfolio average

### Deliverables
1. Truthfulness Scoring Report (3 PDPs)
2. Completeness Scoring Report
3. Evidence Density Analysis
4. Risk Assessment and Mitigation Plan
5. Baseline Scores for Priority SKUs

### Evaluation Criteria
- Accurate scoring methodology (30%)
- Comprehensive gap identification (30%)
- Actionable improvement plan (40%)
