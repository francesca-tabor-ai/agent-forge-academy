# Mathematical Foundations of Modern Supply Chains

## Course Overview

This course reveals, formalizes, and applies the mathematical structures that govern supply chain design, operations, risk, and decision-making.

## Audience

- Supply chain professionals
- Analysts
- Operations managers
- Data scientists
- Graduate-level learners

## Course Goal

To reveal, formalize, and apply the mathematical structures that govern supply chain design, operations, risk, and decision-making.

## Prerequisites

- Calculus
- Probability & statistics
- Linear algebra (recommended)
- Basic optimization (recommended)

## Learning Objectives

Upon completion, students will be able to:

- Translate supply chains into mathematical systems
- Model and forecast demand under uncertainty
- Design optimal inventory policies
- Quantify and manage variability and risk
- Optimize operations flow and capacity
- Solve transportation and routing problems
- Design multi-echelon networks
- Align economic incentives with operational goals
- Apply machine learning to supply chain decisions
- Quantify resilience and optimize for sustainability

## Course Structure

### Module 1: Supply Chains as Mathematical Systems
**Core Question:** How can supply chains be represented, measured, and reasoned about mathematically?

**Key Topics:** Supply chains as networks and flows, state variables, constraints, and objectives, deterministic vs. stochastic systems

**Mathematical Tools:** Graph theory, systems of equations, conservation laws

### Module 2: Demand Modeling & Forecast Mathematics
**Core Question:** How is uncertain demand quantified and predicted?

**Key Topics:** Demand distributions and variability, time series structure, forecast error and uncertainty propagation

**Mathematical Tools:** Probability distributions, ARIMA and state-space models, error metrics (MAE, RMSE, MAPE)

### Module 3: Inventory Theory & Control
**Core Question:** How does mathematics determine how much to stock and when?

**Key Topics:** Tradeoffs between holding, ordering, and stockout costs, single-period vs. multi-period inventory, service-level-based planning

**Mathematical Tools:** EOQ model, newsvendor model, safety stock formulas

### Module 4: Lead Time, Variability & Risk
**Core Question:** How does uncertainty amplify across the supply chain?

**Key Topics:** Lead time distributions, variance amplification (bullwhip effect), risk-adjusted planning

**Mathematical Tools:** Variance propagation, bullwhip effect ratios, Bayesian updating

### Module 5: Operations Flow, Queues & Capacity
**Core Question:** How do congestion and capacity constraints shape performance?

**Key Topics:** Throughput, WIP, and cycle time, bottlenecks and utilization, waiting time explosions

**Mathematical Tools:** Little's Law, queueing models (M/M/1, M/M/c), utilization metrics

### Module 6: Transportation, Routing & Network Optimization
**Core Question:** How are logistics decisions optimized at scale?

**Key Topics:** Transportation cost structures, routing under constraints, multi-node distribution networks

**Mathematical Tools:** Linear programming, Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP), min-cost flow models

### Module 7: Multi-Echelon & Network Design
**Core Question:** How should inventory and capacity be positioned across tiers?

**Key Topics:** Echelon stock vs. local stock, centralization vs. decentralization, network resilience

**Mathematical Tools:** Multi-echelon inventory theory, facility location models, scenario-based optimization

### Module 8: Economics & Incentives in Supply Chains
**Core Question:** How do prices, contracts, and incentives shape behavior?

**Key Topics:** Cost-to-serve, price elasticity, contract design

**Mathematical Tools:** Microeconomic models, elasticity equations, game-theoretic payoff structures

### Module 9: Data Science & Machine Learning Applications
**Core Question:** How do algorithms learn and adapt supply chain decisions?

**Key Topics:** Predictive analytics, classification of risks and failures, policy learning

**Mathematical Tools:** Regression and classification models, reinforcement learning (MDPs), loss functions and optimization

### Module 10: Resilience, Sustainability & System Dynamics
**Core Question:** How do supply chains survive shocks and meet sustainability goals?

**Key Topics:** Disruption modeling, carbon accounting, long-run system behavior

**Mathematical Tools:** Markov chains, carbon intensity equations, dynamic systems modeling

### Capstone Module: Integrated Supply Chain Decision Lab
**Objective:** Apply the full mathematical toolkit to a realistic, end-to-end supply chain case.

**Activities:** Build a demand + inventory + network model, simulate uncertainty and disruptions, optimize cost, service, and risk simultaneously
