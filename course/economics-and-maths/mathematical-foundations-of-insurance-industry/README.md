# Mathematical Foundations of the Insurance Industry: Risk, Pricing, Capital, and Data-Driven Decision Making

## Course Description

This course reveals the mathematical structure underlying modern insurance systems. It integrates probability theory, statistics, optimization, economics, finance, and data science to explain how insurers price risk, manage capital, design products, operate efficiently, and comply with regulation. The course is industry-agnostic across life, health, property & casualty, and specialty lines.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, learners will be able to:

- Translate real insurance problems into mathematical models
- Understand how risk is quantified, priced, and diversified
- Apply statistical and probabilistic tools to claims and underwriting
- Interpret capital, reserving, and solvency metrics
- Evaluate data science and AI methods used in insurance decision-making

## Course Structure

### Module 1: Insurance as a Mathematical System
**Theme:** Why insurance exists and how math makes it viable

**Topics:** Risk pooling and the Law of Large Numbers, random variables and loss distributions, deterministic vs stochastic views, insurance as transformation of uncertainty

**Key Models:** Expected value and variance, frequency–severity decomposition, risk pooling efficiency

### Module 2: Probability Models for Claims
**Theme:** Modeling when losses happen

**Topics:** Claim frequency as a counting process, Poisson and Negative Binomial models, exposure and rate normalization, overdispersion and heterogeneity

**Key Models:** Poisson process, compound distributions, time-to-event modeling

### Module 3: Loss Severity and Extreme Risk
**Theme:** Modeling how big losses can get

**Topics:** Continuous loss distributions, heavy tails and large-loss behavior, Extreme Value Theory, catastrophic vs attritional losses

**Key Models:** Pareto and Lognormal distributions, Generalized Extreme Value (GEV), Exceedance Probability curves

### Module 4: Pricing and Premium Construction
**Theme:** Turning risk into a price

**Topics:** Pure premium and loaded premium, risk loading principles, credibility theory, experience vs exposure rating

**Key Models:** Expected loss + risk load, Bühlmann credibility, Bayesian updating

### Module 5: Statistical Learning for Underwriting
**Theme:** Predicting risk at the individual level

**Topics:** Rating variables and segmentation, Generalized Linear Models (GLMs), regularization and feature selection, model calibration and validation

**Key Models:** Poisson and Gamma GLMs, Elastic Net regularization, Brier score and lift

### Module 6: Reserving and Loss Development
**Theme:** Estimating liabilities that haven't fully emerged

**Topics:** Reported vs ultimate losses, development triangles, Incurred But Not Reported (IBNR), uncertainty in reserves

**Key Models:** Chain Ladder method, Bornhuetter–Ferguson, stochastic reserving

### Module 7: Risk Measures and Capital Adequacy
**Theme:** Ensuring the insurer survives bad years

**Topics:** Surplus processes and ruin theory, Value-at-Risk and Tail Value-at-Risk, coherent risk measures, regulatory capital concepts

**Key Models:** Cramér–Lundberg model, VaR / TVaR, solvency probability

### Module 8: Reinsurance and Risk Transfer
**Theme:** Sharing risk across institutions

**Topics:** Proportional vs non-proportional reinsurance, layers, attachments, and limits, risk diversification, pricing reinsurance contracts

**Key Models:** Excess-of-loss layer formulas, ceded loss distributions, tail risk transfer

### Module 9: Insurance Economics and Behavior
**Theme:** Human behavior under risk and incentives

**Topics:** Moral hazard and adverse selection, deductibles and contract design, demand elasticity, market competition and pricing power

**Key Models:** Utility maximization, game-theoretic incentives, price elasticity formulas

### Module 10: Operations Research in Insurance
**Theme:** Efficiency in claims and underwriting operations

**Topics:** Claims workflow as a queueing system, capacity and staffing optimization, service-level agreements (SLAs), automation vs human decision-making

**Key Models:** M/M/1 queue, cost–service trade-offs, optimization constraints

### Module 11: Investment and Asset–Liability Management
**Theme:** Managing assets to support uncertain liabilities

**Topics:** Time value of money, discounting of claims, duration and convexity, stochastic asset dynamics

**Key Models:** Present value calculations, mean–variance optimization, Brownian motion with drift

### Module 12: Data Science, AI, and Ethics in Insurance
**Theme:** Modern analytics and responsible use

**Topics:** Fraud detection models, causal inference and uplift modeling, fairness and bias metrics, model governance

**Key Models:** Logistic regression, treatment effect estimation, fairness constraints

### Capstone (Optional): Integrated Insurance Model
**Objective:** Build a simplified insurer: pricing, reserving, capital, and profit. Stress test under catastrophe scenarios. Evaluate solvency and profitability.
