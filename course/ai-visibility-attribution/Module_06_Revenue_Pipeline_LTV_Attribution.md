## Lesson 6.1: AI-Assisted Revenue Models

### The Revenue Attribution Challenge

Attributing revenue to AI is complex because:
1. AI often influences early in the journey
2. Multiple touchpoints contribute to conversion
3. Revenue happens days/weeks after AI interaction
4. AI's role may be invisible to traditional tracking

### Model 1: Full Credit (Aggressive)

**How It Works:**
- If AI was in the conversion path, AI gets 100% credit
- No sharing with other channels
- Simple but may overclaim

**Example:**

Journey: AI → Search → Email → Conversion ($1,000)
AI Attribution: $1,000 (100%)

**Pros:**
- Simple to calculate
- Easy to explain
- Maximizes AI's apparent value

**Cons:**
- Overclaims AI's role
- Ignores other channel contributions
- May not be credible to executives

**When to Use:**
- Early-stage AI programs (need to show value)
- Internal advocacy (building support)
- Not recommended for board-level reporting

### Model 2: Proportional Credit (Balanced)

**How It Works:**
- AI gets credit proportional to its role in the journey
- Shares credit with other touchpoints
- More realistic than full credit

**Example:**

Journey: AI (30%) → Search (40%) → Email (30%) → Conversion ($1,000)
AI Attribution: $300 (30%)

**Pros:**
- More realistic
- Acknowledges multi-touch reality
- More credible to executives

**Cons:**
- Requires attribution weight decisions
- More complex to calculate
- May understate AI's value

**When to Use:**
- Standard reporting
- Executive dashboards
- Balanced view of AI impact

### Model 3: Incremental Credit (Conservative)

**How It Works:**
- AI gets credit only for incremental value
- Compares AI-influenced vs. non-AI-influenced outcomes
- Most conservative approach

**Example:**

AI-Influenced Conversion: $1,000
Non-AI Conversion (baseline): $800
AI Incremental Value: $200 (20%)

**Pros:**
- Most conservative and credible
- Defensible to CFOs
- Avoids overclaiming

**Cons:**
- May understate AI's value
- Requires baseline calculation
- More complex methodology

**When to Use:**
- Board-level reporting
- CFO presentations
- When credibility is critical

### Model 4: Assisted Revenue (Hybrid)

**How It Works:**
- AI gets credit for "assisting" conversion
- Partial credit based on AI's role
- Balances realism with value recognition

**Example:**

Journey: AI (assist) → Search (primary) → Conversion ($1,000)
AI Attribution: $200 (20% assist credit)
Search Attribution: $800 (80% primary credit)

**Pros:**
- Recognizes AI's assist role
- Doesn't overclaim
- Realistic and credible

**Cons:**
- Requires role definition (assist vs. primary)
- Subjective decisions needed
- More complex

**When to Use:**
- Balanced reporting
- When AI is clearly assistive (not primary)
- Multi-touch attribution

### Choosing the Right Model

**Decision Framework:**

| Use Case | Recommended Model |
|----------|------------------|
| Internal Advocacy | Full Credit (aggressive) |
| Standard Reporting | Proportional Credit (balanced) |
| Executive Dashboard | Proportional Credit (balanced) |
| Board Presentation | Incremental Credit (conservative) |
| CFO Review | Incremental Credit (conservative) |
| Early-Stage Programs | Full Credit (aggressive) |
| Mature Programs | Proportional Credit (balanced) |

## Lesson 6.2: Pipeline Influence in B2B Contexts

### The B2B Attribution Challenge

B2B sales cycles are long and complex:
- Multiple stakeholders
- Long sales cycles (30-90+ days)
- Multiple touchpoints
- Revenue attribution happens months after AI interaction

### Measuring Pipeline Influence

#### Metric 1: Pipeline Creation

**Definition:** New pipeline created from AI-originated leads

**Calculation:**

AI Pipeline Created = Sum of all opportunities created from AI-originated leads

**Example:**
Month 1: 50 AI-originated leads
Average Deal Size: $50,000
Pipeline Created: 50 × $50,000 = $2,500,000

#### Metric 2: Pipeline Velocity

**Definition:** How quickly AI-originated deals move through pipeline

**Calculation:**

AI Pipeline Velocity = Average days from lead to closed-won for AI-originated deals

**Example:**
Non-AI Deals: 90 days average
AI-Originated Deals: 60 days average
Velocity Improvement: 33% faster

#### Metric 3: Win Rate

**Definition:** % of AI-originated opportunities that close

**Calculation:**

AI Win Rate = Closed-Won AI Opportunities / Total AI Opportunities

**Example:**
AI Opportunities: 50
Closed-Won: 20
Win Rate: 40% (vs. 25% for non-AI)

#### Metric 4: Average Deal Size

**Definition:** Average value of AI-originated deals

**Calculation:**

AI Average Deal Size = Total Revenue from AI Deals / Number of AI Deals

**Example:**
Total AI Revenue: $1,000,000
Number of Deals: 20
Average Deal Size: $50,000 (vs. $40,000 for non-AI)

### Pipeline Attribution Model

**Multi-Stage Attribution:**

| Stage | AI Attribution Weight | Rationale |
|-------|----------------------|-----------|
| Lead Creation | 40% | AI creates qualified demand |
| Qualification | 20% | AI pre-qualifies |
| Opportunity Creation | 20% | AI influences opportunity |
| Closed-Won | 20% | AI supports closing |

Total: 100% across pipeline stages

**Example:**

AI-Originated Deal: $100,000
Lead Creation: $100,000 × 40% = $40,000
Qualification: $100,000 × 20% = $20,000
Opportunity: $100,000 × 20% = $20,000
Closed-Won: $100,000 × 20% = $20,000

Total AI Attribution: $100,000 (100% across stages)

### Real-World B2B Example

**SaaS Company - AI Pipeline Impact:**

**Pipeline Metrics:**
- AI Leads: 200/month
- Conversion to Opportunity: 30% (60 opportunities)
- Win Rate: 40% (24 closed-won)
- Average Deal Size: $50,000
- Total Revenue: $1,200,000/month

**vs. Non-AI Leads:**
- Non-AI Leads: 500/month
- Conversion to Opportunity: 20% (100 opportunities)
- Win Rate: 25% (25 closed-won)
- Average Deal Size: $40,000
- Total Revenue: $1,000,000/month

**AI Impact:**
- Higher conversion rate (30% vs. 20%)
- Higher win rate (40% vs. 25%)
- Larger deals ($50K vs. $40K)
- **Result:** 200 fewer leads, but $200K more revenue

## Lesson 6.3: LTV Lift from Pre-Qualified AI Demand

### Why AI Users Have Higher LTV

AI pre-qualifies users before they arrive:
1. **Better Product Fit:** AI matches users to right products
2. **Realistic Expectations:** AI sets expectations before purchase
3. **Education:** AI educates users about products
4. **Qualification:** Only qualified users click through

### Measuring LTV Lift

#### Method 1: Cohort Comparison

**How It Works:**
- Compare AI-originated cohort to non-AI cohort
- Measure LTV over time (6, 12, 24 months)
- Calculate lift

**Example:**
```
12-Month LTV:
AI Cohort: $850
Non-AI Cohort: $600
LTV Lift: $250 (42% increase)
```

#### Method 2: Regression Analysis

**How It Works:**
- Build regression model
- Control for other factors
- Isolate AI's impact on LTV

**Example:**
```
LTV = Base LTV + (AI_Flag × $250) + Other_Factors

AI Impact: $250 LTV lift (statistically significant)
```

#### Method 3: Matched Comparison

**How It Works:**
- Match AI users to similar non-AI users
- Compare LTV between matched pairs
- Calculate average lift

**Example:**
```
Matched Pairs: 1,000
Average LTV Lift: $200
Statistical Significance: p < 0.01
```

### LTV Attribution Calculation

**Attributing LTV to AI:**
```
AI LTV Attribution = 
  (AI User LTV - Baseline LTV) × Number of AI Users

Example:
AI User LTV: $850
Baseline LTV: $600
LTV Lift: $250
Number of AI Users: 1,000
Total AI LTV Attribution: $250,000
```

### Real-World LTV Example

**E-commerce Company - AI LTV Impact:**

**Cohort Analysis (12 months):**
- AI-Originated Users: 5,000
- Average LTV: $850
- Total LTV: $4,250,000

- Non-AI Users: 20,000
- Average LTV: $600
- Total LTV: $12,000,000

**LTV Lift:**
- Per User: $250 (42% increase)
- Total: $1,250,000 (5,000 users × $250)

**ROI Calculation:**
- AI Investment: $500,000
- LTV Lift: $1,250,000
- ROI: 150%

## Lesson 6.4: Conservative vs Aggressive Attribution Methodologies

### The Credibility Spectrum

Attribution methodologies range from conservative (credible but may understate) to aggressive (maximizes value but may lack credibility).

#### Conservative Approach

**Characteristics:**
- Incremental credit only
- Requires statistical significance
- Compares to baseline
- Defensible to CFOs

**Example:**
```
AI Revenue: $1,000,000
Baseline Revenue (without AI): $800,000
AI Incremental: $200,000 (20%)

Conservative Attribution: $200,000
```

**Pros:**
- Highly credible
- Defensible
- Avoids overclaiming
- CFO-approved

**Cons:**
- May understate value
- Requires baseline calculation
- More complex

#### Aggressive Approach

**Characteristics:**
- Full credit for AI-influenced conversions
- No baseline comparison
- Maximizes apparent value
- Simple calculation

**Example:**
```
AI-Influenced Revenue: $1,000,000

Aggressive Attribution: $1,000,000 (100%)
```

**Pros:**
- Simple
- Maximizes value
- Easy to explain
- Good for advocacy

**Cons:**
- May overclaim
- Less credible
- May be challenged
- Not CFO-approved

#### Balanced Approach

**Characteristics:**
- Proportional credit
- Multi-touch attribution
- Realistic but recognizes value
- Credible to most stakeholders

**Example:**
```
AI-Influenced Revenue: $1,000,000
AI Attribution Weight: 30%

Balanced Attribution: $300,000 (30%)
```

**Pros:**
- Realistic
- Credible
- Recognizes AI value
- Balanced

**Cons:**
- Requires weight decisions
- More complex
- May still be challenged

### Choosing the Right Approach

**Decision Matrix:**
```
Stakeholder | Recommended Approach
------------|----------------------
CFO | Conservative (incremental)
CMO | Balanced (proportional)
CEO | Balanced (proportional)
Board | Conservative (incremental)
Internal Team | Aggressive (full credit)
Early Stage | Aggressive (full credit)
Mature Program | Balanced (proportional)
```

### Hybrid Approach

**Use Multiple Models:**
- **Internal:** Aggressive (build support)
- **Standard Reporting:** Balanced (realistic)
- **Executive:** Balanced (credible)
- **Board:** Conservative (defensible)

**Example:**
```
Same Revenue: $1,000,000

Internal Report: $1,000,000 (aggressive)
Standard Dashboard: $300,000 (balanced)
Board Presentation: $200,000 (conservative)
```

## Practical Exercise 1: Revenue Influence Model

### Objective
Build a revenue attribution model that ties AI influence to commercial outcomes.

### Steps

#### Step 1: Choose Attribution Model (30 minutes)

1. **Define Use Case:**
   - Who is the audience? (CFO, CMO, Board, Internal)
   - What's the purpose? (Advocacy, Reporting, Decision-Making)
   - What's the risk tolerance? (Conservative, Balanced, Aggressive)

2. **Select Model:**
   - Full Credit (Aggressive)
   - Proportional Credit (Balanced)
   - Incremental Credit (Conservative)
   - Assisted Revenue (Hybrid)

#### Step 2: Define Attribution Rules (45 minutes)

1. **Create Attribution Logic:**
   ```javascript
   function calculateAIRevenue(conversion, attributionModel) {
     if (attributionModel === 'aggressive') {
       // Full credit if AI in path
       return conversion.value;
     } else if (attributionModel === 'balanced') {
       // Proportional credit
       const aiWeight = getAIWeight(conversion.path);
       return conversion.value * aiWeight;
     } else if (attributionModel === 'conservative') {
       // Incremental credit
       const baseline = getBaselineRevenue(conversion);
       return conversion.value - baseline;
     }
   }
   ```

2. **Define Attribution Weights:**
   - If using proportional: What % for AI?
   - If using assisted: What's assist vs. primary?
   - If using incremental: What's the baseline?

#### Step 3: Calculate Revenue Attribution (60 minutes)

1. **Gather Data:**
   - All conversions with AI in path
   - Conversion values
   - Attribution paths
   - Baseline data (if incremental)

2. **Apply Attribution Model:**
   - Calculate AI revenue for each conversion
   - Sum total AI revenue
   - Calculate AI revenue share

3. **Validate Results:**
   - Do numbers make sense?
   - Are they defensible?
   - Do they align with expectations?

#### Step 4: Create Reporting (30 minutes)

1. **Define Metrics:**
   - Total AI Revenue
   - AI Revenue Share (% of total)
   - AI Revenue per User
   - AI Revenue Trends

2. **Build Dashboard:**
   - Revenue by attribution model
   - Revenue trends over time
   - Revenue by AI platform
   - Revenue by product/category

### Deliverables

1. **Attribution Model:** Selected model with rationale
2. **Attribution Rules:** Complete logic and weights
3. **Revenue Calculations:** AI revenue by model
4. **Reporting Framework:** Metrics and dashboard design

### Evaluation Criteria

- **Appropriateness:** Model matches use case and audience
- **Accuracy:** Calculations are correct
- **Defensibility:** Results can be explained and defended
- **Actionability:** Results inform decisions

## Practical Exercise 2: Finance-Aligned Attribution Narrative

### Objective
Create a narrative that explains AI attribution in terms finance executives understand and trust.

### Steps

#### Step 1: Understand Finance Perspective (30 minutes)

1. **Identify Finance Concerns:**
   - Overclaiming
   - Lack of proof
   - Inconsistent methodology
   - Unclear ROI

2. **Define Finance Language:**
   - Incremental revenue
   - Statistical significance
   - Baseline comparison
   - ROI calculation

#### Step 2: Build Finance Narrative (60 minutes)

1. **Structure Narrative:**
   ```
   Introduction:
   - What we're measuring
   - Why it matters
   - Methodology overview
   
   Methodology:
   - Attribution approach (conservative)
   - Baseline definition
   - Statistical validation
   - Assumptions and limitations
   
   Results:
   - AI revenue (incremental)
   - AI ROI
   - Confidence intervals
   - Comparison to baseline
   
   Conclusion:
   - Key takeaways
   - Recommendations
   - Next steps
   ```

2. **Use Finance Language:**
   - "Incremental revenue" not "AI revenue"
   - "Statistically significant" not "we think"
   - "Baseline comparison" not "full credit"
   - "ROI" not "value"

#### Step 3: Create Supporting Materials (45 minutes)

1. **Build Financial Dashboard:**
   - Revenue by month
   - Incremental revenue
   - ROI calculation
   - Cost breakdown

2. **Create Documentation:**
   - Methodology document
   - Assumptions document
   - Validation results
   - FAQ document

#### Step 4: Test Narrative (15 minutes)

1. **Present to Finance:**
   - Share narrative
   - Gather feedback
   - Address concerns
   - Refine as needed

### Deliverables

1. **Finance Narrative:** Complete story in finance language
2. **Supporting Materials:** Dashboard and documentation
3. **Feedback Summary:** Finance team feedback and refinements

### Evaluation Criteria

- **Clarity:** Finance team understands the narrative
- **Credibility:** Finance team trusts the methodology
- **Defensibility:** Narrative can withstand scrutiny
- **Actionability:** Narrative leads to decisions

## Key Takeaways

- **Multiple revenue models exist:** Full credit, proportional, incremental, assisted
- **Choose model by audience:** Conservative for CFOs, balanced for executives, aggressive for advocacy
- **B2B requires pipeline metrics:** Pipeline creation, velocity, win rate, deal size
- **LTV lift is significant:** AI users have 20-50% higher LTV
- **Credibility matters:** Conservative approaches are more defensible
- **Finance alignment is critical:** Use finance language and conservative methodologies
